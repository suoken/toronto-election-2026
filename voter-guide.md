# Toronto 2026 Municipal Election — Voter Guide

**Election day: Monday, October 26, 2026** — polls open 10 a.m.–8 p.m. Bring one piece of ID with your name and Toronto address. Find your assigned voting place through MyVote at toronto.ca/elections. Three races on the ballot: mayor, ward councillor, school trustee.

This guide compares — it does not recommend or endorse any candidate. Every claim is attributed to a source; where something could not be verified, that is stated explicitly rather than filled in.


## Part A — The Mayoral Race: Olivia Chow vs. Brad Bradford

**Election day: Monday, October 26, 2026** (polls open 10 a.m.–8 p.m.)

### The race at a glance

- **Olivia Chow**, the incumbent mayor (in office since the 2023 byelection), is seeking a second term on a record of property-tax-funded service expansion.
- **Brad Bradford**, councillor for Ward 19 (Beaches–East York) since 2018 and former vice-chair of the Budget Committee under John Tory, is the main challenger, running on affordability through tax relief and spending restraint.
- **Chris Alexander** (former federal Conservative cabinet minister) suspended his campaign on **October 6, 2026**, saying polls showed no viable path to victory; he declined to endorse either candidate. Because the withdrawal came after the city's deadline, his name remains on the ballot. (Sources: Wikipedia "2026 Toronto mayoral election" poll table; particle.news, Oct 6, 2026.) Other names on the ballot include candidates polling in the low single digits such as Sara McVie and Odessa Paloma Parker (Mainstreet Research, Oct 6–7, 2026, via Towhey's newsletter).

### 1. Housing affordability and supply

#### Positions

**Olivia Chow** — A city-led, public development model. Since taking office, 5,271 city-owned housing units have broken ground (including 1,680 affordable units), with a goal of 65,000 rent-controlled homes by 2030; she promised shovels in the ground in 2027 on 10,000 new rental homes. She cut development charges (DCs) for projects up to six units, created a DC-eliminating incentive for purpose-built rentals, and secured the $1.5 billion tri-government deal (June 2026) to lower DCs 40–60% through 2029. She champions as-of-right multiplexes and faster approvals (claims Toronto is 80% faster at approving housing), plus tenant protections including a proposed $2.15-million bad-landlord task force under RentSafeTO. She supports increasing taxes on vacant and luxury homes to fund housing, and **opposes** cutting the municipal land transfer tax, saying the roughly $850 million a year it raises pays for city services. (Sources: storeys.com, Oct 2026; rentalhousingbusiness.ca, Oct 8, 2026; toronto.ca news release, June 23, 2026; Fox Marin YouTube summary.)

**Brad Bradford** — Cut transaction and building costs to unlock supply. His headline pledge: eliminate the municipal land transfer tax on the first $1.1 million of a home purchase (saving buyers up to ~$18,000), which he argues will increase market mobility and rental supply through competition rather than mandates. He wants to reduce DCs, parkland payments and approval times (Build Canada candidate questionnaire), concentrate density growth on major streets and near rapid transit, and permit housing primarily by removing city-imposed barriers. (Sources: heartify.life election primer, Oct 8, 2026; tobj.ca, Oct 5, 2026; buildcanada.com candidate questionnaire.)

### 2. TTC / transit (safety, fares, service levels)

#### Positions

**Olivia Chow** — Fare freezes and rider affordability: TTC fares frozen for three consecutive years (2024–2026), with a promise of a fourth freeze in 2027; monthly fare capping after 47 paid rides introduced September 2026 (≈2.1 million free rides), with a promise of "free after 10 paid rides in a week." She points to the Scarborough Busway (opened late September 2026, replacing Line 3) as delivery on transit for the east end. Her 2026 budget expanded service hours and funded mental-health crisis workers and additional safety workers for the TTC. On congestion: expand transit, improve intersection signal technology, deploy traffic agents. (Sources: toronto.ca 2026 budget news release, Feb 10, 2026; heartify.life, Oct 8, 2026.)

**Brad Bradford** — A "15-minute TTC service guarantee or your money back"; no fare-price commitment until a line-by-line audit is complete. Prioritize the Scarborough East LRT as the next major transit project. Police presence on the TTC and platform edge doors (see his Safer Subways motion below). On congestion: remove red-painted bus lanes on Kingston Rd., Morningside Ave., Kennedy Rd. and Midland Rd. to open road space for drivers. In the Build Canada questionnaire, he prioritized state-of-good-repair spending and automobile capacity/curbside parking when street space is contested. (Sources: heartify.life, Oct 8, 2026; buildcanada.com questionnaire.)

### 3. Public safety and crime

#### Positions

**Olivia Chow** — Points to her record of hiring more police officers and first responders; the 2026 budget funded 360 more paramedics, 156 new firefighters, and a multi-year plan for police to hire 720 new officers, plus TTC mental-health crisis workers and safety workers. She says she would consult local communities on their safety needs and has worked with Toronto Police to enhance safety measures for Jewish communities amid rising antisemitic incidents. In the Oct 8 CBC debate, when pressed on choosing not to take her seat on the Police Services Board, she said she has confidence the board is doing a good job under chair Shelley Carroll. (Sources: heartify.life, Oct 8, 2026; Downsview Advocate 2026 budget summary; CBC debate via newsbeep.com.)

**Brad Bradford** — Promises to hire 1,000 new police officers and to have police presence on the TTC; advocates mandatory/structured treatment for people experiencing addiction; "make parks safe for residents and their pets." His Build Canada questionnaire answer: increase the police budget above inflation to expand staffing/services, and allow police and approved organizations to deploy automated licence-plate readers under existing privacy law. (Sources: heartify.life, Oct 8, 2026; CBC debate via newsbeep.com; buildcanada.com questionnaire.)

### 4. Property taxes and city budget

#### Positions

**Olivia Chow** — Promises future property-tax increases "at or around" the rate of inflation, and told the Oct 8 CBC debate she would not raise taxes more than ~1% above inflation. Her record: 9.5% (2024, highest in 25 years), 6.9% (2025), 2.2% (2026). She says the increases filled a $1.8 billion post-pandemic budget hole, that the city's credit rating improved to AA+, and that a line-by-line review already happens annually (finding ~$700 million in savings in the 2026 budget). She wants luxury property owners and speculators to contribute more (vacant-home tax, luxury home tax). (Sources: CBC debate via newsbeep.com; CTV via canadianbusinessnews.org; toronto.cityhallwatcher.com.)

**Brad Bradford** — Promises a one-year property-tax freeze in his first year, then increases below inflation. He would launch an independent line-by-line review of city spending (first since 2011), restructure Toronto Water into a taxpayer-owned utility to cut water bills 25%, and finance infrastructure with long-term debt rather than pay-as-you-go ("modernize" city finances, as the province and Toronto Hydro do). He notes renters also bear property taxes indirectly through rent. (Sources: taxpayer.com newsroom; CBC debate via newsbeep.com; heartify.life, Oct 8, 2026.)

### 5. Bike lanes and traffic congestion

#### Positions

**Olivia Chow** — Defends council's authority over bike lanes and opposed the province's Bill 212 (which empowered removal of protected lanes on Bloor St., Yonge St. and University Ave.). City staff estimated removal would cost ~$48 million and not reduce congestion. Her office reiterated that municipal infrastructure decisions should rest with council. On congestion more broadly: expand transit, signal technology, traffic agents. (Sources: cyclingmagazine.ca; Cycle Toronto; toronto.ca.)

**Brad Bradford** — Sides with drivers and the province on reallocating road space. He voted against council's core opposition to Bill 212 and moved (unsuccessfully) to support restoring two motor-vehicle lanes in each direction on Bloor St. W. (Runnymede Rd. to Resurrection Rd.) even if it requires removing or reconfiguring cycling infrastructure. He has cited the Etobicoke Bloor section (38,490 cars/day vs. 197 bikes) as misallocated space. Note: he originally voted in favour of the Bloor St. bike-lane expansion as a councillor. He also proposes removing red-painted TTC bus lanes in Scarborough to open road space. (Sources: toronto.ca, Agenda Item History 2024.CC23.1; europesays.com/CityNews, Oct 2026; heartify.life.)

### 6. Homelessness and shelters

#### Positions

**Olivia Chow** — A "caring," housing-first approach she says is working: more shelter beds, Rent Bank funding, supportive housing and drop-in centres; she cites Financial Accountability Office figures showing 1,730 fewer people on Toronto's homelessness list between 2022-23 and 2024-25. Her campaign statement: "Olivia Chow is building more shelter beds and homes to get people off the streets and put a roof over their heads, and she will continue to do so." (Sources: CBC via europesays.com, Oct 2026; tobj.ca, Oct 5, 2026.) Toronto spends about $786 million a year on shelters. (CBC Toronto, Oct 7, 2026.)

**Brad Bradford** — Treats homelessness through public safety and public-health integration: fund and build at least 100 dedicated recovery-house beds, structured/mandatory addiction treatment for people with severe dependencies in public spaces, and prioritized supportive and transitional housing; leans on other governments for addiction help. He argues housing alone cannot solve chronic homelessness without addressing substance dependency and mental-health trauma, and rejects simply adding "more bureaucracy." (Sources: tobj.ca, Oct 5, 2026; CBC via europesays.com, Oct 2026.)

### 7. Cost of living

#### Positions

**Olivia Chow** — Affordability through service spending: lower grocery prices (food-security measures), expanded free school meal programs (breakfasts and lunches), longer library hours (all 100 branches, 7 days a week from summer 2026), TTC fare freezes and fare capping, property-tax relief/deferral programs for seniors and people with disabilities, and asking "luxury property owners and speculators to contribute more." (Sources: buildups.ca, Oct 2026; toronto.ca 2026 budget news release; CTV via canadianbusinessnews.org.)

**Brad Bradford** — Affordability through tax and fee relief: one-year property-tax freeze (then below inflation), eliminating the municipal land transfer tax on the first $1.1M of a home purchase (up to ~$18,000 saved), cutting water bills 25%, and a 15-minute TTC service guarantee (or money back). No fare-price commitment pending a line-by-line audit. (Sources: heartify.life, Oct 8, 2026; taxpayer.com newsroom.)

### Notes, gaps, and caveats

- **Candidate lists:** verified against the City Clerk's certified list as republished by Wikipedia (citing the official list; page crawled Oct 19, 2026) and spot-checked against votetoronto.com and onac.ca ward pages. The City's own candidate-list page (toronto.ca) is JavaScript-driven and could not be text-extracted by the available tools. Confirm the final ballot at toronto.ca/elections or via MyVote.
- **Voting records:** all votes above are from the City Clerk's published recorded votes on secure.toronto.ca (council agenda item histories). "Yes"/"No" reflects the councillor's vote on the motion as adopted or defeated; "Absent" means not recorded as voting (may include late arrivals/departures).
- **Ward 4 and Ward 11** have no incumbent running; all candidates are marked as first-time council candidates (Mike Layton previously served 2010–2022 but has no votes in the Oct 2024–Oct 2026 window).
- **Challenger positions:** verified only where stated above with a named source (campaign sites or documented candidate comparisons). All other challengers are marked "no public position found" — campaign materials were not systematically reviewed for every candidate. Endorsements listed are documented public endorsements (Labour Council, Progress Toronto, ABC Toronto, CUPE Local 79, ATU Local 113, No Demovictions) and are not positions.
- **Scope limits:** tables cover key headline motions only, not exhaustive roll-calls. Notable motions outside the required categories (e.g., the March 2025 councillor pay raise, the June 2025 protest "bubble zone" bylaw, the Nov 2024 shelter-infrastructure plan) were not included. Per-councillor votes on individual 2026-budget amendment motions beyond those shown are available in the agenda item histories linked below.

### Key motions referenced — quick guide

All vote counts below are the City Clerk's recorded votes from secure.toronto.ca unless noted.

1. **Bike lanes — Bill 212 response (2024.CC23.1, Nov 13–14, 2024).** Council voted 21–4 to oppose the province's Bill 212 amendments stripping municipal control of bike lanes and to direct the City Solicitor to explore litigation (Chow motion, parts 1–5). A separate motion by Brad Bradford to support restoring two vehicle lanes on Bloor St W (Runnymede–Resurrection) even if it required removing bike lanes lost 6–18.
2. **Property tax — 2025 budget levy (2025.CC27.1, Feb 11, 2025).** Council adopted the tax rates funding Mayor Chow's 2025 budget 19–5 (No: Bradford, Burnside, Holyday, Pasternak, Perruzza; Saxe and Crisanti absent). Residential increase: 5.4% + 1.5% City Building Fund levy = 6.9% total (Storeys; City decision text).
3. **TTC budget — 2025 budget, TTC portion (2025.CC27.1, Feb 11, 2025).** Adopted 22–1 (only Holyday opposed; Saxe absent, Colle declared an interest, Crisanti absent).
4. **TTC service — RapidTO transit priority lanes, Dufferin & Bathurst (2025.EX25.4, Jul 23–24, 2025).** Dedicated bus/streetcar lanes adopted 20–3 (No: Bradford, Holyday, Pasternak; Cheng and Colle declared interests and did not vote). On the Bathurst-specific recommendations (3–4), the vote was 18–5, with Burnside joining the No votes.
5. **Housing — Rental Renovation Licence (renoviction) by-law (2024.PH16.4, Nov 13–14, 2024).** Adopted 25–1 (only Holyday opposed); all ward-14–25 incumbents in office at the time voted Yes. Effective July 31, 2025.
6. **Housing — as-of-right sixplex pilot (2025.PH22.4, Jun 25–26, 2025).** Council adopted zoning to allow low-rise sixplexes as-of-right on a pilot basis (Toronto & East York District plus specified Scarborough lands) 18–6. No votes: Crisanti, Holyday, **Kandavel**, Pasternak, Perruzza, **Thompson**. A Holyday deferral motion lost 17–7 (Burnside voted Yes to defer).
7. **Shelter — six new shelter sites (2025.PH23.3, Jul 23–24, 2025).** Council approved zoning amendments for six new municipal shelter sites (68 Sheppard Ave W, 1615 Dufferin St, 2204–2212 Eglinton Ave W / 601 Caledonia Rd, 2535 Gerrard St E, 66–66Y Third St, 1220–1222 Wilson Ave) 22–3 (No: Bradford, Holyday, Pasternak). All ward-14–25 incumbents in office voted Yes; the Ward 25 seat was vacant.
8. **2026 budget (Feb 10, 2026).** Council passed Mayor Chow's $18.9B operating budget with a 2.2% combined residential property-tax increase (0.7% operating + 1.5% City Building Fund) and a third consecutive TTC fare freeze. Reported as unanimous (Toronto Sentinel; Downsview Advocate); City of Toronto news release confirms adoption.

## Methodology and limitations

**Voting records** cover October 2024–October 2026 and come from the City of Toronto's published council decisions and recorded votes (City Clerk, via secure.toronto.ca agenda items). Tables show key headline motions per issue — bike lanes, TTC budget and fares, housing, property taxes, shelters/encampments — not exhaustive roll-calls.

**Candidate positions** come from campaign websites and platforms, the October 15 TVO/Rotman mayoral debate, and reputable news outlets (CBC, Toronto Star, CP24). Positions are current as of October 19–20, 2026.

**Candidate lists:** the City's own candidate directory at toronto.ca/elections is JavaScript-driven and could not be read by text extraction in this research pass. Ward lists were taken from the Wikipedia "2026 Toronto municipal election" page, which cites the City Clerk's certified candidate list (nominations closed August 21, 2026; 361 candidates certified August 24), spot-checked against votetoronto.com and onac.ca ward pages. Confirm your ward's final list at toronto.ca/elections before voting.

**What's missing or limited:**
- Challenger (first-time candidate) positions were not systematically reviewed across ~190 council candidates; "no public position found" means unverified for this guide, not that none exists.
- A few individual council votes could not be verified from published sources (e.g., individual votes on the 2024/2025/2026 budget property-tax portions beyond reported tallies; one agenda page returned HTTP 403). These are flagged inline.
- No full transcript of the October 15 TVO debate was retrievable; debate claims come from news summaries and the published chapter list.
- Ward 19 (Beaches–East York) is an open seat: incumbent Brad Bradford is running for mayor.
- Polls cited are public polls conducted October 6–14, 2026; newer polls may exist by election day.
- School trustee races are not covered in this guide.
