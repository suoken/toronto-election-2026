Olivia Chow is making neighborhoods safer with her investments in faster 911, policing and youth programs

Chow is re-opening 54 Division in East York by 2030 to keep communities safe

BEACHES-EAST YORK — Olivia Chow says making communities safer is critical as she shared her plan to hire more police and first responders, continue to reduce 911 wait times and support youth with violence prevention programs.

Chow highlighted her investment in rebuilding the shuttered 54 Division in East York and announced it will reopen in 2030. Chow funded a new standalone 54 Division as mayor.

“When you love something, you care for it. And when you care for something, you protect it,” said Chow. “Caring means protecting people. It means investing in the services that keep our neighbourhoods safe. And it means listening when a community tells you something isn't working — and then doing something to fix it.”

She spoke about community safety alongside Beaches-East York residents who say their concerns will be addressed by the restoration of the local police station in their community.

Olivia Chow has a comprehensive plan for community safety:

-

Maintaining and improving 911 call wait times, which have improved by 90% during Chow’s time as mayor.

-

Investing in the multi-year hiring plan for police, which hires 360 officers per year until 2029, which is the maximum number allowable by the province.

-

Investing the multi-year hiring plan for the Toronto Paramedic Services, adding 331 paramedics to the service by 2028.

-

Under Chow, Toronto Fire hired 156 new firefighters

-

Supporting youth with mentorship, jobs and violence prevention, including increasing funding for the Mayor’s Youth Safety Task Force that funds locally-developed programs throughout Toronto

-

Maintaining and expanding her investment in Toronto Community Crisis Service which provides trained mental health workers for people in crisis, city-wide

Chow said her investments are part of her commitment to building a safer, more affordable and more caring Toronto — and warned that cutting services would put that progress at risk.

“This election is a choice between moving forward together to keep our city safe, or going backwards with cuts to the services families depend on,” said Chow.

BACKGROUND

54 Division funding:

-

54 Division closed in 2018, leaving communities north of Danforth and south of Eglinton served by 55 Division at Dundas and Coxwell. Residents have spent years calling for its return, citing concerns about auto theft, break-ins and police responsiveness.

-

Chow’s 2025 Capital Budget included $120.2 million to de-amalgamate 54 and 55 Divisions and fund a new standalone 54 Division. See the Toronto Police Budget Note.

-

The new 69,000-square-foot division will be nearly three times the size of the original facility, with operational space for 60 officers per shift, community meeting facilities and modern police infrastructure. Design contracts were approved in 2026, and the project is included in the City's 10-year capital plan.

Frontline Services hiring plans

-

Olivia Chow funded the Toronto Police Service’s 5-year hiring plan, which began in 2025 and hires the maximum number of police officers each year, as per the provincial training allocation. Four classes of 90 new officers each year.

-

Under Chow, Toronto Fire hired 156 new firefighters as part of a 3-year hiring plan to increase service and reduce response time

-

Olivia Chow funded a new 4-year Hiring Plan for Toronto Paramedic Services, adding 331 paramedics to the service by 2028.

-

Olivia Chow established Toronto Community Crisis Response as Toronto’s Fourth Emergency Service, available city-wide and now embedded on the TTC.
