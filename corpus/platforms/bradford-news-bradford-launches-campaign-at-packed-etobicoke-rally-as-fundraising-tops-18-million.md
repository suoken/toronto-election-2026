Title: BRADFORD LAUNCHES CAMPAIGN AT PACKED ETOBICOKE RALLY AS FUNDRAISING TOPS $1.8 MILLION
Published: 2026-07-07
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

At his first campaign rally, Bradford tells supporters “Toronto is stuck” and frames the October vote as a change election.

July 7, 2026 (Etobicoke, Ontario) Toronto mayoral candidate Brad Bradford held his first campaign rally tonight at the Ukrainian Huculak Centre in Etobicoke, addressing a capacity crowd of supporters and casting the October 26th election as a choice between accepting a city that has stalled and demanding one that works.

Ahead of the rally, the campaign announced it has surpassed $1.8 million in fundraising, one of the best resourced fundraising efforts in the race.

“This is a change election, and the choice could not be clearer,” said Bradford. “Toronto is stuck: Stuck in traffic. Stuck on waitlists. Stuck paying more and getting less. A city led by Mayor Chow means more crime, more congestion, and more cost. I’m running to make our streets safe again, to get this city moving, and to give families real relief on the cost of living, so we can build a city we can be proud of again.”

Bradford opened the evening alongside his wife, Kathryn, and paid tribute to the volunteers and families who built and sustained the Ukrainian community centre, holding it up as an example of the civic spirit he says Toronto is at risk of losing.

The campaign also announced its co-chairs: conservative strategist and public affairs entrepreneur Mike Van Soelen, and former journalist and Liberal strategist Genevieve Tomney.

“This election is a simple choice,” said Bradford. “Between settling for the way things are, and demanding something better, now. I’m running because I believe this city is ready for change, and I believe it can’t wait.”

Notables in attendance included Etobicoke Lakeshore MP James Maloney, St. Paul’s MPP Stephanie Smyth, Etobicoke Centre Councillor Stephen Holyday, former Etobicoke MPP Donna Cansfield, and former Etobicoke Councillor John Campbell.
