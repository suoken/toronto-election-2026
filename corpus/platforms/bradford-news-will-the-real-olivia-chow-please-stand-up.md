Title: Will the Real Olivia Chow Please Stand Up?
Published: 2026-10-18
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

Half of this city rents. For three years, NDP Mayor Olivia Chow has told them she is on their side.

Today the Bradford for Mayor campaign is asking why the Mayor says one thing to one room and a different one to another, with a recording showing that the Mayor changes her message depending on who she’s talking to.

While the Chow campaign and her official surrogates at Progress Toronto continue to make up lies about Brad Bradford, our campaign is asking a legitimate question: what is Olivia Chow’s position on renters? She said one thing to one audience and a different one to another. The original interview is here.

Our video setting her two answers side by side is available to see here on X/Twitter, YouTube, and Instagram.

In a video produced for Chinese-language media outlet 51.ca, in which Chow speaks in English with Chinese subtitles, she tells landlords: “If they have terrible renters that won’t pay their rent, the City of Toronto will support them.” She calls it “outrageous that a landlord have to wait so long on the tribunal to get an approval,” and says “the provincial government must see this as a priority because it’s just not fair as a landlord if someone have wrecked your place, will not pay rent, that you are not able to evict them.”

Who is Olivia Chow really fighting for – landlords or renters? Torontonians deserve to know.

She tells renters that she will protect them and will not tolerate slumlords. In front of a different audience, she tells landlords she’s on their side and that fairness requires faster evictions.

When speaking to renters, she opposes the provincial government’s Bill 60 because it harms tenants by speeding up evictions. When speaking to landlords, she supports Bill 60 because it currently takes too long for a landlord to evict a tenant.

She opposed the province speeding up evictions. Then she demanded the province do more to speed up evictions. Both of those cannot be true.

Renters deserve to know which one she means.

BACKGROUNDER: Olivia Chow on landlords and renters: what she said to landlords vs what she’s telling renters

What she's telling landlords (time-stamps from this video, published by a Chinese-language channel in October 2026, in which the Mayor speaks in English):

-
“We think it is outrageous that a landlord have to wait so long on the tribunal to get an approval. We have said to the provincial government they really need to speed up the system.” (8:13)

-
“The provincial government must see this as a priority because it’s just not fair as a landlord if someone have wrecked your place, will not pay rent, that you are not able to evict them.” (8:35)

What she's telling renters:

-
Opposing Bill 60, which would “make it easier for landlords to evict renters by cutting notice periods in half, cutting the options for requesting an eviction be postponed, and hiring more enforcement officers to forcibly remove renters from their homes.” (Letter to Council, Oct 21, 2025)

-
“Bill 60 makes it easier for landlords to evict tenants, weakens appeals rights and cuts compensation.” “That is not how we build housing, and it is not how we build a caring city.” (Global News, Nov 4, 2025)

-
“Half of Toronto's residents rent their homes. They deserve stability, fairness, and the peace of mind that comes from knowing they would not face an unfair eviction or sky-high rent increases. My first key item will be my motion that opposes the province's Bill 60, which makes it easier for landlords to evict tenants, weakens appeal rights, and cuts compensation for people forced to leave their homes.” (CityNews, November 12, 2025, 0:34-1:05)

-
“The rest of Bill 60 still includes an erosion of renters’ rights at the LTB.” (X, Oct 26, 2025)
