Title: OLIVIA CHOW SAYS SHE FOUND $788 MILLION IN SAVINGS. HER BUDGET IS UP NEARLY $3 BILLION. WHICH IS IT?
Published: 2026-09-26
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

Bradford releases the numbers behind the Mayor's "savings" claim: only $135 million is actual spending reduction. The rest is fee hikes, money from other governments, deferrals and accounting. Where did the money go?

TORONTO, ON | Brad Bradford today put a simple question to Olivia Chow. If you found $788 million in savings, why is your budget nearly $3 billion bigger than the one you inherited?

When Olivia Chow took office in 2023, the city's operating budget was $16.16 billion. The budget she passed this year is $18.9 billion. That's $2.7 billion more, roughly 17 per cent, in 3 budgets. At the same time, the Mayor has told Torontonians she found $788 million in "efficiencies, reductions and offsets." Both claims can't be true at once unless the word "savings" means something different at City Hall than it does at your kitchen table.

It does. The City's own budget documents break the $788 million down, and the breakdown tells the story:

-
$135 million is actual spending reduction: $57 million in efficiencies and $78 million from internal line by line reviews. That's 17 cents on the dollar.

-
$264 million is a hiring slowdown and "cost deferrals." Money not spent this year that gets spent next year, after the election.

-
$286 million is "revenue adjustments": user fee increases of 2 to 5 per cent on everything from permits to recreation programs, plus money from the federal and provincial governments. Charging you more isn't a saving.

-
$103 million is assessment growth and other adjustments. New buildings paying property tax for the first time isn't a saving either.

And even that wasn't enough. To close a $1.34 billion hole, the Mayor pulled another $323 million out of reserves.

"Every family in Toronto knows what a saving is. It's when you spend less than you did before," said Bradford. "Olivia Chow spent $2.7 billion more, raised your fees, took more money from other governments, put off bills until after the election and called it $788 million in savings. That's not finding money. That's hiding where it went."

This is the pattern of the Chow years. The province has never given Toronto more money: roughly $9 billion over 10 years under the 2023 New Deal, the Gardiner Expressway and the Don Valley Parkway taken off the city's books, more than $1.2 billion in new operating funding. Olivia Chow took all of it and raised your taxes anyway. In 2023 she promised property tax increases would be "modest." Then she delivered 9.5 per cent – the largest increase in the city’s history – then 6.9 per cent, then an election-year 2.2 per cent she only got that low by draining $1.7 billion from the city's reserves. That's nearly 20 per cent compounded, almost $700 more a year on the average home.

More money from the province than ever. Higher taxes than ever. A budget nearly $3 billion bigger. And what did it buy? A record 4.1 million food bank visits last year, up 340 per cent since 2019. The worst traffic congestion in North America. A Union Station no world city would accept as its front door. A downtown square people avoid after dark.

At the Daily Bread Food Bank debate, Olivia Chow raised her paddle and said Toronto is living up to its full potential. Bradford rejects that. We're not even close. And the money to do better already came through the door.

"I'm a city planner by training. When something isn't working, you open it up and look under the hood. You don't put a new label on the old problem," said Bradford. "The Mayor found $135 million in real savings in a budget of nearly $19 billion and called it $788 million. That tells you all you need to know. I’ll bring in independent, outside experts to go through the Budget, line by line. Every division, every agency, every program, with the first real review since 2011. And until that review shows you where your money is going, your property taxes are frozen in year one."

"Toronto isn't broken. It's stuck," said Bradford. "Stuck is what happens when a mayor spends $3 billion more and tells you she saved you money. On October 26, Torontonians can choose change: a city that's safe, a city you can afford, a city that moves. A Toronto we can be proud of."

BACKGROUND: THE $788 MILLION, BY THE CITY'S OWN NUMBERS

Source: City of Toronto, 2026 Capital and Operating Budget Launch presentation and Mayor's 2026 Budget report.

Operating budget, 2023 (the budget in place when Chow took office): $16.16 billion. Operating budget, 2026: $18.9 billion. Increase: $2.74 billion.

2026 budget pressure to balance: $1.34 billion ($1.072 billion opening pressure plus $268 million in unanticipated pressures).

The $788 million in "efficiencies, reductions and offsets":

-
Reductions, $135 million: efficiencies ($57 million) and line by line reviews ($78 million).

-
Program and agency reviews, $264 million: hiring slowdown for non-frontline positions and cost deferrals.

-
Revenue adjustments, $286 million: user fee increases of 2 to 5 per cent, investment earnings, federal and provincial funding updates.

-
Other adjustments, $103 million: assessment growth revenue, automated speed enforcement changes.

Additional measures to balance: budget bridging from reserves and 2025 surplus, $323 million; financing adjustments, $193 million.

Property tax record: 2024, 9.5 per cent. 2025, 6.9 per cent. 2026, 2.2 per cent, bought with $1.7 billion drained from reserves. Compounded: nearly 20 per cent, almost $700 a year on the average home.
