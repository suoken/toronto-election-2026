Olivia Chow is Creating Nearly 40,000 Jobs

Chow’s rental incentives create good jobs and build more homes

SCARBOROUGH — Olivia Chow’s housing plan is getting rental homes built and supporting nearly 40,000 jobs in Scarborough and across Toronto.

Joined by Jason Ottey of LIUNA Local 183 at 26 Gilder Drive, Chow said support for rental construction helps keep skilled tradespeople working while building homes people need.

“When we get housing moving, we get people working. Here in Scarborough and across our city, that means a paycheque that covers rent, buys groceries and helps a family get ahead,” said Chow. “Our affordable housing projects support almost 40,000 jobs. These are livelihoods. These are families.”

When rising construction costs and a slowing market threatened to leave shovel-ready housing projects on hold, Chow got those shovels in the ground. Her rental incentives program creates construction jobs and delivers more affordable homes.

Chow is also working with Prime Minister Mark Carney and the Province to build on that progress. Toronto recently secured up to $1.5 billion through the Canada-Ontario Partnership to Build, giving the City greater financial certainty to reduce development charges and help unlock the next generation of rental housing. The agreement shows what can happen when leaders work together. .

The complex at 26 Gilder was the first project to break ground and now – just three years later – is almost ready. It delivers 341 rental homes, including 80 affordable homes, close to Kennedy Station, jobs and services.

The benefits extend beyond the construction site. Building homes supports suppliers and neighbourhood businesses. Once residents move in, they become customers at local shops and contribute to the community. More rental housing also gives young people a better chance to stay in Scarborough when they leave their parents’ home.

Chow will keep working to help create good jobs and continue building a city that is safe, caring and affordable.
