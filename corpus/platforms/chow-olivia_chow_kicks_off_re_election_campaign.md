TORONTO - Olivia Chow’s launched with energy and momentum at the Danforth Music Hall tonight, with nearly 1,000 supporters in attendance, live performances by local artists, and speakers who shared stories about working with Olivia over the years to better the city we love.

The event featured performances by:

- Jim Cuddy, Canadian music icon and co-founder of Blue Rodeo

- Sunthar V, internationally touring comedian and writer from Scarborough

- DijahSB, acclaimed Toronto-based rapper

- Pan Fantasy Steelband, one of Toronto’s most celebrated steel pan ensembles

And remarks by:

- Randell Adjei, Ontario’s first Poet Laureate

- Laurel Rothman, dedicated social work leader and longtime advocate who worked alongside Olivia.

- Ryan Endoh, tenant organizer, ACORN leader and paralegal who lives at 500 Dawes Road

See below for Olivia Chow’s Rally Speech:

Toronto – are you ready to choose hope over fear? Unity over division? To move forward not backward? Are you ready to win?

I love Toronto! I love the vibrancy and energy from the millions of people who come to our street festivals – from all over the world. From every culture, heritage and language.

I love skating. I learned to skate at Nathan Phillips Square. It made me feel like a Canadian, like I belonged here. I loved that it was, and still is, free.

I learned how to ride a bike on Centre Island. I spent a lot of time at the Reference Library. I used old art books to build up my career as a struggling artist.

I love kayaking on our lake. It will always be Lake Ontario!

Our city made me who I am today. An immigrant kid from St Jamestown who became your mayor. And all of my best memories of our city happened in public spaces. Places we all created together.

All of the opportunities I had growing up happened because our city made them possible. Public spaces and services make our city great.

But today, we have challenges we need to solve, together.

Far too many kids are lined up with their moms at the food bank. Too many go to bed hungry. Too many can’t concentrate at school because their bellies are empty and grumbling.

I remember the exact moment it became my mission to deal with child hunger. On a Christmas Eve 35 years ago, after dinner with my late husband Jack Layton and our family. We thought of all the children who would not have such an opportunity to have a holiday feast.

Together Jack and I dreamed about a plan to feed every kid in every school in Toronto. We calculated how much it would cost. We mapped out a detailed plan. We worked hard at City Hall to give children the best chance to be successful in life.

So it is with great joy, after 35 years, now as your mayor, finally, that program Jack and I dreamed of is a reality. In every classroom. In every school. For every child.

And we’re going to keep going. We are going to build more affordable housing. We are going to keep our streets safe. We are going to support public transit and make it even more affordable!

All of these make our city better, stronger. They give every generation of kids and every family a chance. No matter if you’re from downtown, Scarborough, Etobicoke, North York, or any of the awesome neighbourhoods in our city.

When I first arrived at city hall as your mayor. No affordable housing was being built. That was a choice. I am making a different choice.

Today, thousands of new affordable homes are under construction. 7 out of 10 cranes in the sky are there because of the work we are doing.

When I first arrived at city hall three years ago, TTC riders were angry because their fares had gone up and their service had been cut.

That’s just not right.

As your mayor, I did the exact opposite. I kept the fares low, and I’m investing in faster service.

Back then, many libraries were closed on Sundays and evenings. Today, all 100 libraries are open 7 days a week with longer hours.

Back then, 911 wait times were well over the national average. Today, our city is safer. 911 call times are 90% faster. We have more first responders. Including mental health crisis workers city wide.

Toronto, we have turned the corner, and we are feeling more optimistic. We have momentum. Now we have to keep that momentum going. Because these are not easy times. Trump and his tariffs create uncertainty and stress. Our world is rapidly changing around us. Everything is less affordable, for almost everyone..

So we have a choice to make.

We can choose to work together, support each other, continue building the city we love, a joyous city that invites us and welcomes us. Where we care for each other and stand up for each other.

Where we celebrate who we are: ambitious, hardworking, ready to win, ready to go forward. Where we build more housing and lower the cost of living. Where we decide our own future. As Torontonians.

Or we can go backwards. We could choose leaders who will starve our public spaces and cut our services. Divide us instead of bringing us together. That is not what I want. I do not believe it’s what you want either.

We love our city and we know we can build an even more caring, more affordable, and safer Toronto. Together.

Where a mom shopping for groceries does not have to put anything back. Because our city is saving her money by feeding kids in school. Where you can count on public transit and fares are low, so more of us can be where we need to be.

Where slum lords no longer get a free ride, and are now fixing their long neglected buildings, so renters can live in dignity.

Where seniors can age in their home and their own neighbourhood.

Where your local government continues to lower your cost of living by building more affordable housing, faster.

Where grocery chains don’t rip you off by snooping on your shopping habits and raising the price.

We have built the foundation. Let’s finish what we started. Let’s choose to be confident and hopeful. Let’s choose to make our city more affordable. Let’s choose to care. Let’s choose to keep moving forward and keep building a Toronto we love, together.
