Title: PEOPLE’S FEELINGS AREN’T STATISTICS: BRADFORD MEETS WITH RESIDENTS IMPACTED BY WEEKEND GUN VIOLENCE
Published: 2026-07-14
Issue category: Crime

Mayoral candidate says that when families stop going to festivals because they are scared, the answer from City Hall can’t be a spreadsheet

TORONTO — After a weekend in which more than a dozen people were shot across Toronto, three of them fatally, in three separate shootings in North York, at the Salsa on St. Clair festival, and at Polson Pier. Mayoral candidate Brad Bradford today sat down with residents, parents, and business owners directly impacted by the violence, including the shooting at Salsa on St. Clair that killed two people in a crowd of 13,000.

Bradford opened the conversation with a single question for every participant: how are you feeling?

"I spent the weekend on St. Clair and on the phone with people across this city, and I asked everyone the same question: how are you feeling," said Bradford. "More than a dozen people were shot in this city this weekend, and the response from City Hall was to call it a ‘difficult weekend’ and tell us the city is extremely safe. But people don’t conduct their lives according to statistics. Nobody checks a dashboard before they take their kids to a festival. They ask one question: do I feel safe?"

"When a family stops participating in the life of this community because they’re afraid, the answer can’t be a statistic. It has to be action," said Bradford. "A city that is not safe is not free."

Bradford also rejected voices at City Hall suggesting that Salsa on St. Clair, one of the largest celebrations of Latin culture in the country, should be scaled back or cancelled.

"The answer to violence is never that families lose their festival. Salsa on St. Clair has to come back next summer: big, joyful, and safe," said Bradford. "The people with the strollers do not surrender this city to the people with the guns."

"The men and women who ran toward the gunfire on Saturday deserve our gratitude and our support," said Bradford. "My quarrel isn’t with them. It’s with political leadership that hides behind their numbers instead of standing behind their work."

Bradford committed that as mayor he would treat public safety as the first job of City Hall, making whatever changes are required, from changes to the law to changes to leadership, to get the city back to what it should be, and measuring success not only by incident counts but by whether residents in every part of the city actually feel safe on their streets, on transit, and at their festivals.

"I would rather lose this election than stand in front of people after a weekend like that and tell them everything’s fine," said Bradford. "It’s not fine. We have a big problem to fix. Fixing it starts with a leader who listens to how people feel instead of telling them what to think. No more weekends like the one we just had. That’s my promise."
