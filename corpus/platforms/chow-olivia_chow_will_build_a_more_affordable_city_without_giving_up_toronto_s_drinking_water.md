Olivia Chow will build a more affordable city without giving up Toronto’s drinking water

Chow rejects any scheme to privatize water, which drives up costs and puts safety in question

TORONTO - Olivia Chow will build a more affordable city without handing over our drinking water to the province, or opening the door to privatization.

In the final stretch of the campaign, Chow is reminding voters about the stakes in this election, saying she will always protect public services, especially when it comes to our precious drinking water. She was joined by acclaimed scientist and broadcaster David Suzuki and local families at RC Harris Water Treatment Plant.

Today, 16 incumbent Toronto City Councillors from across the political spectrum have spoken out to oppose Bradford’s plan.

According to a new legal analysis, Bradford’s scheme hands over control of Toronto’s water to the provincial government, taking away the City’s power to set rates, decide infrastructure priorities, and making the City powerless against privatization.

“There is a lot at stake in this election. My opponent is proposing to give up control of Toronto’s safe, clean drinking water. We wouldn’t be able to set the rates, decide which pipes need upgrades, or even stop privatization by the Ford government. I will never gamble with a public service that every family depends on, and I will never open the door to privatization,” said Chow.

“I have a plan to lower people’s day-to-day costs and make life more affordable in our city. Lowering transit costs, supporting local grocery stores and bringing more affordable food into our neighbourhoods, building more affordable homes, so our children have a chance to build their lives here. These are the things Torontonians are asking for, not privatizing water, which will hurt safety and drive up costs like it has in other cities around the world,” said Chow.

Already, the Toronto Region Board of Trade is advocating for Bradford’s proposed water corporation to be opened up to private investment.

BACKGROUND:

Water legislation and legal opinion:

-

According to a new legal analysis of provincial legislation by Goldblatt Partners, the province would have the ultimate authority to set the water rates (making it impossible for any candidate to guarantee low rates), as well as control over ownership, governance and operations.

-

The province would also have control over privatizing water, the legal opinion states:

The Bradford Proposal states that a WWC would be owned 100% by Toronto taxpayers and a bylaw would permanently ban private investment. This is misleading. City Council can pass a bylaw, but does not have the power to change or over-ride the Ministerial powers to intervene in WWCs. If the Minister decided to change the ownership or control of a WWC, they would have the power to do so. See page 2.

Opposition from City Councillors and the public

-

16 incumbent Toronto City Councillors from all sides of the political spectrum have signed a letter against Bradford’s water scheme:

“Using debt to pay for water infrastructure is a bad idea. It will cost Torontonians more in the long run” said incumbent Jon Burnside, Don Valley East.

-

Former Mayor David Miller has spoken out against Bradford’s scheme here. He writes:

Yet one conservative mayoral candidate, Brad Bradford — and the conservative think tank backing him — want to turn Toronto Water into a separate corporation. That is the first step toward privatization: an ideological answer to a problem that does not exist. And it is, above all, a really, really bad idea.

-

A poll shows 62% of Torontonians say they are concerned about the possibility of Toronto Water being privatized.
