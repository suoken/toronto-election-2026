Title: BRADFORD UNVEILS CAMPAIGN VISION FOR “A TORONTO WE CAN BE PROUD OF AGAIN”
Published: 2026-05-06
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

Campaign website goes live at bradford26.ca

TORONTO – Toronto City Councillor and candidate for Mayor, Brad Bradford today launched his campaign’s website at bradford26.ca and set out the foundational vision of his campaign: a Toronto residents can be proud of again.

The website launch follows Bradford’s nomination filing on Friday, May 1, the first day of the nomination period. Bradford was one of the first in the door at the City Clerk’s Office. Mayor Olivia Chow has not filed her own nomination papers, and despite repeated questions from reporters, has declined to confirm whether she will seek re‑election.

“I filed on Friday because Toronto deserves a campaign in the open, on the public record, and frankly not on the public dime. The Mayor of this city has not done that. She has been asked, more than once, whether she is running. She will not say. You cannot use the Mayor’s Office, the Mayor’s Budget, the Mayor’s calendar, and the Mayor’s staff to do the work of a campaign and then refuse to admit it is a campaign. Toronto needs better than that and once again, the Mayor is asking Torontonians to accept less than what they deserve. On October 26, Torontonians will have a choice: keep what you have got or demand better.” – Brad Bradford

Bradford also called on the City and on Mayor Chow to address the emergence of third parties attacking his candidacy on Facebook and in the news, including by groups that have not registered as a third party under the City’s election rules.

“There is a third party in this race posting attacks against me without registering as a third party. The City has rules. The Mayor is silent. If the people posting these attacks want to be in this race, they can register and play by the rules like everyone else. That is the standard.” – Brad Bradford

Bradford framed today’s launch as an answer to two questions Torontonians keep asking: what kind of city are we becoming, and who is going to do the work to fix it. He said pride in Toronto cannot be declared from City Hall. It has to be earned, block by block, service by service, day by day.

The campaign continues to be anchored by three priorities. The Three C’s: Crime, Congestion, and Cost. Bradford said these are not abstractions or talking points. They are the issues every Torontonian feels every day, and they are the ones a Mayor is elected to deliver on.

Crime, because the confidence Torontonians once felt walking home, riding the subway, or watching their kids in the park has been quietly eroded, and the city cannot afford to keep pretending it has not. Congestion, because the time tax City Hall levies on residents, in road closures no one coordinates and transit that does not run on time, climbs higher every year. Cost, because families and small businesses are being asked to pay more for a city that delivers less.

Toronto is in a competition we cannot afford to lose. For talent. For investment. For the families deciding where to put down roots and the businesses deciding where to grow. Every time Toronto loses a headquarters, or a festival, or a young family to Calgary or Austin or Miami, we lose a little more of the Toronto we built. A Toronto worth being proud of.

“Torontonians give this city their time, their talent, and their treasures. The deal is supposed to be that the city gives something back. That deal has been broken. We are going to fix it.” – Brad Bradford

The launch of bradford26.ca marks the formal beginning of the campaign’s public engagement phase. The site sets out the candidate’s biography, the campaign’s issue framework, and portals for volunteers, donors, and supporters. Detailed policy announcements will follow over the coming weeks, with plans tied to each of the campaign’s priorities.

“I am running for Mayor because I believe in this city. I believe Toronto can be the best city in the world to raise a family, build a business, and unlock your potential. That is the goal. And we are going to build a Toronto we can all be proud of again.” – Brad Bradford

Brad Bradford has served as Toronto City Councillor for Ward 19, Beaches–East York, since 2018. He filed his nomination for Mayor of Toronto on Friday, May 1, 2026. A trained urban planner with a background in environmental studies, he was raised in Hamilton, Ontario. He and his wife Kathryn are raising their two daughters, Briar and Bronwyn, in the East End.
