Olivia Chow’s Vision for a More Caring, Affordable and Safer City

Chow says Toronto needs to keep moving forwards, not backwards

ST. JAMES TOWN (October 23, 2026) - Olivia Chow spoke with families in St. James Town about her vision for a Toronto that’s more caring, affordable and safer. She stood outside the rental building where she grew up, in the neighbourhood that shaped her principled decades-long career standing up for the people of our city.

“My mother worked as a hotel maid. She earned enough to pay the rent and put food on the table here in St. James Town. It wasn't easy, but the City worked for us. I won’t stop fighting until it works for you too,” said Chow. “People deserve a mayor who has their back. I have a plan to bring your costs down, to make our neighbourhoods more safe, to stand up for people and our city no matter what challenges we face.”

Chow shared her perspective from her years of public service, as a school trustee, metro councillor, Toronto city councillor, a member of parliament and now as mayor.

“I've seen entire generations of kids grow up in our city. I’ve seen new communities sprout up. New neighbourhoods begin to shine. Our skyline fill out. I know the decisions we make today will shape the city our children and grandchildren inherit," said Chow.

“This election is a choice. Do we keep moving forward, together, to make life more affordable? Or do we go backwards? Backwards to TTC cuts and higher fares. Backwards to giving away control of our water. Backwards to giveaways for the few, and cuts for everyone else,” said Chow. “I have made my choice. I will keep standing up for families across our city, keep us moving forwards, and keep building a more caring, more affordable and safer city.”

OLIVIA CHOW'S FULL SPEECH - Check against delivery

We often say in our city “Home is Toronto.” Well, behind us here is my first home in Toronto.

I came here when I was 13 years old. And right here, in St. James Town, my family found an affordable apartment. My father struggled with mental illness and could not work. But my mother worked as a hotel maid. She earned enough to pay the rent. Put food on the table. And give us a home.

It wasn't easy. But Toronto was a place where second chances could happen and new lives could be made. A place where an immigrant kid from St. James Town could grow up to become your mayor. And I've never forgotten that.

Because I know firsthand how much it can mean, when someone in a position to make a difference, truly cares what happens to you.

I've spent my life standing up for the people of Toronto. As a school trustee. A metro councillor. A Toronto councillor. A member of parliament. And now, as your mayor.

I've seen entire generations of kids grow up in our city. I’ve seen new communities sprout up. New neighbourhoods begin to shine. Our skyline fill out. Teams win championships, and teams wait for their time to shine again.

And I’ve learned that the decisions we make today will shape the city our children and grandchildren inherit.

Our city has changed a lot. But in all the important ways, I have not.

I’m still the kid from St James Town who is in awe of our city and love for its people, and who has dedicated herself to helping others, to lifting people up, and listening.

That's the kind of mayor I've always tried to be. Never perfect but always focused on the greater good. And that's behind my vision for Toronto.

A more caring, more affordable and safer city. Where everyone belongs.

These are not easy times. When I meet you on the street, I hear the same worries my family had, right here. I know you feel it when you tap your PRESTO card. When you buy groceries. When the rent comes due. When you’re stuck in traffic. These costs come every day. Every week. Every month. And you deserve a mayor who has your back, and who has a plan to bring those costs down.

I have that plan.

Lower transit fares. Lower grocery costs. More homes you can afford.

Under my plan, after 10 TTC rides in a week, the rest are free. Faster, cheaper and safer TTC.

More affordable local grocery stores. More competition. And stopping price gouging.

Feeding more kids in schools. More free children's programs in our libraries.

Helping seniors stay in the homes they love as they age.

15,000 homes under construction. Thousands of them affordable. And I'll build more.

Protecting renters. Cracking down on bad landlords. Making sure apartments are repaired. Because a home should be a place of security. And a caring city must also be a safe city. We've added more than 1,000 police officers and crisis workers. 911 response is twice as fast.

And we're not done. I'll keep investing in safer neighbourhoods. Supporting young people. And protecting the people we love. And I’ll never privatize our water. Never.

That's what caring looks like. Understanding what families are going through. And doing something about it.

This neighbourhood was where I fell in love with Toronto and where I decided to make a difference. And everything I am I owe to my mother, who believed in Toronto. Because Toronto gave her every reason to. Our city worked for us. Which is why I won't stop fighting until it works for you too.

So this election is a simple choice: Do we keep moving forward, together, to make life more affordable?

Or do we go backwards? Backwards to TTC cuts and higher fares. Backwards to giving away control of our water. Backwards to giveaways for the few, and cuts for everyone else. Why risk it? That’s not what Toronto wants. And it’s what I’ve fought against every day of my life in public service.

So, I have made my choice. I will keep standing up for families across our city. And I will keep bringing costs down.

This race is close. Your vote will decide it. Our city depends on it. So please, vote on Monday, October 26. Bring a friend. Bring a neighbour. And when you do, remember there are two Chows on the ballot, and if you want to keep making progress, it's me, Olivia Chow, number nine.

Let's keep moving forward, together. For a more caring, affordable and safer city.
