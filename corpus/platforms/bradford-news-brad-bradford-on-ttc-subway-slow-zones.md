Brad Bradford on TTC Subway Slow Zones: Response to Olivia Chow

Published October 20, 2026

Has Olivia Chow previously committed to eliminating subway slow zones?

The campaign has found no record of Olivia Chow ever committing to eliminate slow zones. In August 2026, she said: “For the TTC, especially for maintenance and for capital projects, I rely on engineers. I rely on experts. I don’t want to second-guess.” (Toronto Today, August 13, 2026) If Mayor Chow has made that commitment before, she should point to it.

How many slow zones has the TTC had under Mayor Olivia Chow?

The TTC has imposed 296 slow zones since Olivia Chow became mayor. The number peaked at 34 at the same time in summer 2024, and about a dozen remain today. That covers three years and three city budgets.

What happened to TTC track inspections during the World Cup?

Under Mayor Chow, a subway track inspection was deferred so the system would look good during the FIFA World Cup. Within weeks after the tournament, the number of slow zones climbed back to 18.

Is the City of Toronto making record investments in the TTC?

The TTC’s own state-of-good-repair plan has a $37 billion funding gap. More than $1 billion of the City of Toronto’s capital budget went unspent last year. Those numbers do not support Mayor Chow’s claim of record investment.

How do slow zones affect TTC riders?

Toronto subway riders deal with early closures, routine weekend shutdowns, and trains slowed to 15 kilometres per hour in slow zones. Mayor Chow has accepted this as the status quo.

What is Brad Bradford’s plan to eliminate TTC slow zones?

As mayor, Brad Bradford will:

-
Set the standard for subway slow zones at zero.

-
Publish the slow zone count every month, with a reason and a repair date for every slow order.

-
Order an independent audit of TTC subway track within his first 100 days in office.

-
Launch a Subway Track Renewal Program, starting in the 2027 capital budget.

How will Brad Bradford pay for the Subway Track Renewal Program?

The program will be paid for by modernizing the City budget. It requires no new taxes and no service cuts.

When can Toronto voters choose a new mayor?

The Toronto municipal election is on October 26, 2026. Toronto isn’t broken. It’s stuck, and voters can fix it by changing the mayor.
