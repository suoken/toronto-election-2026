Title: OLIVIA CHOW SAYS TORONTO IS LIVING UP TO ITS FULL POTENTIAL. BRADFORD SAYS NO: THE FAMILIES WHO BUILT THIS CITY DESERVE BETTER.
Published: 2026-09-17
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

Bradford: the promise of Toronto is that if you work hard and play by the rules, you can be anything. 4.1 million food bank visits later and a 20 percent tax increase - that promise is broken.

TORONTO, ON | At Monday night's mayoral debate, hosted by the Daily Bread Food Bank, candidates were asked a simple yes or no question: is Toronto living up to its full potential? Mayor Olivia Chow raised her sign and said yes. This morning at Victor’s home in Malvern, Brad Bradford gave a different answer.

"Toronto was built, and is being built, by people who gave up everything to get here," said Bradford. "Doctors, pharmacists, professors, and accountants like Victor. They left their careers and the lives they knew in every corner of the earth so their children would have a crack at the promise of Canada. The promise of Toronto. Work hard, play by the rules, and you can be anything. Ask those families whether that promise is being kept today."

The year Mayor Chow calls Toronto at its full potential saw a record 4.1 million visits to Toronto food banks, up 340 per cent since 2019, with more than 1 in 10 residents now relying on one to get through the month (Who's Hungry 2025, Daily Bread Food Bank and North York Harvest). Property taxes went up almost 20 per cent. Parents check playgrounds for needles before their kids can play. Too many weekends end with a shooting on the news. And the same families are paying more and getting less.

"When the Mayor says Toronto is living up to its full potential, she's telling you to settle," said Bradford. "Settle for the lineups. Settle for the needles. Settle for the sirens. I won't, and I don't believe the families of this city will either. We aren't living up to our potential, and we aren't living up to our part of the bargain."

Bradford said Monday's debate put two very different visions of Toronto on one stage: a mayor who believes this is as good as it gets, and a plan for change that gets a stuck city moving again. His plan tackles the three things Torontonians raise most at the door: crime, congestion and the cost of living, including 1,000 new police officers in his first term, more voluntary and mandatory treatment options for people whose addiction has taken over their lives, a new Scarborough Eglinton East LRT under construction by 2030, platform edge doors on the TTC subway, and getting the bureaucracy out of the way so homes get built.

"Toronto isn't broken. It's stuck," said Bradford. "The people who chose this city kept their end of the deal. On October 26th, Torontonians can choose a mayor who delivers. That's the change I'm offering: a city that's safe, a city you can afford, a city that moves. A Toronto we can be proud of."
