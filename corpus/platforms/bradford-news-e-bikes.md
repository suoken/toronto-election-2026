Title: BRADFORD DECLARES CRACKDOWN ON DANGEROUS E-BIKES
Published: 2026-05-20
Issue category: Congestion

Mayoral candidate Brad Bradford calls for motor-style e-bikes to be licensed and insured as part of eight-point public safety package

TORONTO. City Councillor and mayoral candidate Brad Bradford today released a comprehensive proposal targeting the growing threat of motor-style, out-of-class electric bikes on Toronto streets, sidewalks, cycle tracks, and in residential buildings.

Bradford's plan is part of his commitment to make Toronto a city we can be proud of again: a city that is safe for pedestrians, cyclists, and motorists, and one that takes congestion seriously.

A growing class of two-wheeled electric mini-motorcycles, many of them styled as sport bikes, are being operated on Toronto streets, sidewalks, cycle tracks, and multi-use trails without licences, insurance, or plates.

The core of the proposal would ensure that these mini-motorcycles are licensed and insured, as any other motor vehicle on our roadways would be. Bradford’s plan also calls for police to be given the power to seize and impound illegally modified e-bikes.

The danger is not only on Toronto streets. It is in Toronto homes. Toronto Fire Services has reported a substantial year-over-year increase in lithium-ion battery fires associated with personal mobility devices in residential buildings.

“A pedal welded to a mini motorcycle does not make it a bicycle. A senior on the sidewalk, a parent in the bike lane, and a family in an apartment building deserve more than a City Hall that has chosen willful ignorance over leadership. As Mayor, I will use every tool the City already has, and I will work in good faith with the Province and Ottawa, so that public safety in this city means something again.”

— Brad Bradford

While Council will deliberate on an open provincial consultation this week and has asked City staff to return with by-law amendments by January 2027, Torontonians deserve a council and a mayor who take action now.

The eight-point package combines actions the City can take today under its existing authority with targeted requests of the Province of Ontario and the federal government.

“Under Mayor Chow, we have been asked to accept that motorcycles on the sidewalk and battery fires in our buildings are just the price of a growing city. They are not. Innovation does not have to come at the cost of public safety. Toronto deserves a mayor who addresses these concerns, not one who waits for someone else to do it. That is not leadership.”

— Brad Bradford

BACKGROUND

Under Ontario's Highway Traffic Act and O. Reg. 369/09, a lawful power-assisted bicycle must meet strict criteria: functional pedals, a motor of 500 watts or less, a 32 km/h speed cut-off, a maximum unladen weight of 120 kg, and certified brakes. Devices that fail any of those tests are not lawful e-bikes. Many are nonetheless sold, modified and operated on our streets.

Toronto Fire Services has reported a substantial year-over-year increase in lithium-ion battery fires associated with personal mobility devices in residential buildings. The Province has opened ERO Posting 026-0422 for public comment until June 7, 2026. Toronto City Council has directed staff to return with by-law amendments in2027. Bradford argues the City cannot wait.

THE EIGHT-POINT PACKAGE

-
Fast-track changes to the Toronto Municipal Code to ban mini-motorcycles and other motorized vehicles in bike lanes, cycle tracks, painted lanes, and multi-use trails.

-
Call on the Province to amend the Highway Traffic Act to authorize Toronto Police and designated by-law officers to seize and impound illegally modified out-of-class motorized devices at the curb.

-
Request courier and transportation businesses operating in Toronto to educate contractors on the penalties associated with illegal modifications to e-bikes and the requirement to comply with all rules of the road.

-
Recommend the Province adopt the two-class e-bike definition and add an appearance-based exclusion, modelled on Quebec's Highway Safety Code, that disqualifies motorcycle and moped-styled devices regardless of label, based on visible features such as footrests, body panels, non-adjustable saddles, and motorcycle-style wheels.

-
Stand up a Toronto E-Bike Safety Task Force, jointly led by Toronto Fire Services and Municipal Licensing and Standards, with authority to inspect retailers, publish quarterly safety data, and suspend or revoke licences for repeat violators.

-
Require a Toronto Safe Riding point-of-sale notice as a binding condition of e-bike retailer licensing, turning an existing education recommendation into an enforceable obligation.

-
Request the Province mandate top-tier safety certification (UL 2849 and UL 2271) for e-bike electrical systems and batteries sold in Ontario.

-
Call on the federal government to set mandatory lithium-ion battery safety standards for imported e-bikes, e-scooters, and their batteries and chargers under the Canada Consumer Product Safety Act, and to give the Canada Border Services Agency clear authority and the tools it needs to detain and refuse non-compliant shipments at the border.
