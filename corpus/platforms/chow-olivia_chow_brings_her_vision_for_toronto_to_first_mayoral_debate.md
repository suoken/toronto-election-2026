TORONTO (September 14, 2026) — Olivia Chow took the stage tonight in front of a packed room at the Daily Bread Food Bank for the first mayoral debate of the campaign. She delivered a clear message: Toronto has turned a corner, and now is the time to keep moving forward, not go backwards. Torontonians saw a strong, poised Mayor, the same steady leadership that has guided City Hall through difficult times. In the face of uncertainty, Chow made clear that steady leadership, not chaos or division, is what Toronto needs right now.

Chow laid out her record of making Toronto more affordable, more caring, and safer: expanding school food programs now reaching 330,000 kids and saving families up to $900 a year, speeding up 911 response times, and investing in frontline workers and youth. She reminded Torontonians that when she took office, TTC fares were rising and service was falling. She did the opposite: freezing fares and boosting service for transit riders.

Chow also spoke to standing up for Toronto families, from cracking down on bad landlords to stopping grocery giants from price gouging shoppers, and pointed to ending over a decade of stalled affordable housing construction. She highlighted her track record working with other levels of government, citing the confidence she's earned from Prime Minister Carney and Premier Doug Ford, while making clear she's never been afraid to stand up for the city when needed.

She drew a clear contrast with her opponents, pointing to her decades long fight for programs like school food since her time as a city councillor and MP, work she has now delivered as Mayor. Others on the stage struggled to point to anything they'd delivered for Torontonians.

Olivia Chow will continue campaigning across the city in the days ahead, sharing her vision for a more caring, more affordable, and safer Toronto for all.
