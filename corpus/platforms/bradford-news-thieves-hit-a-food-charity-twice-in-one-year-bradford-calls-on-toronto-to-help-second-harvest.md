Title: THIEVES HIT A FOOD CHARITY TWICE IN ONE YEAR. BRADFORD CALLS ON TORONTO TO HELP SECOND HARVEST
Published: 2026-10-12
Issue category: Cost of Living

7 trucks that deliver rescued food to charities across the city are out of service after catalytic converters were stolen over the Thanksgiving weekend. Donate at secondharvest.ca.

TORONTO, ON | Brad Bradford visited Second Harvest's truck yard in Etobicoke today, where 7 of the charity's delivery trucks sit idle after thieves cut a hole through the fence over the Thanksgiving weekend and stole the catalytic converters from under them. The break-in took minutes. The repairs will take 2 to 3 weeks and cost about $80,000, money that was meant to go to rescuing good food and getting it to people who need it.

Second Harvest is Canada's largest food rescue organization. Its trucks collect surplus food from grocers, producers and distributors and deliver it to more than 500 charities across Toronto, from food banks and shelters to school programs and seniors' centres.

It's the second time this year the fleet has been targeted. On New Year's Day, four trucks were sabotaged and taken out of service.

"This is a charity that feeds people, and someone crawled under its trucks in the middle of the night and took them off the road," said Bradford. "Food bank visits in Toronto are up 340% since 2019. There were 4.1 million visits last year alone. The food on these trucks was headed to the people behind those numbers. They're the ones who got robbed this weekend."

Bradford said the theft reflects a pattern residents across the city recognize. Homicides and shootings are down this year, and the credit belongs to the men and women of the Toronto Police Service. But auto theft, break and enters, thefts over $5,000 and major crime overall are up in 2026, according to the Toronto Police Service's own numbers.

"Catalytic converters, stolen cars, kicked-in doors, smashed storefronts. These get called small crimes, and they aren't small to the people on the receiving end," said Bradford. "Somewhere along the way this city got used to it. I don't accept that. A charity feeding hungry people shouldn't have to budget for thieves. A city that is not safe is not free."

Today, Bradford's ask is simpler: help Second Harvest get its trucks moving again.

"Second Harvest has been a lifeline for this city for decades, and right now it needs one back," said Bradford. "Every dollar donated goes to repairing these trucks and getting food to the charities counting on them. Toronto isn't broken. It's stuck. And the way we get unstuck is by looking after each other. If you can give, please give."

Donations to Second Harvest can be made at secondharvest.ca/donate.

Anyone with information about the theft can contact Toronto Police or Crime Stoppers anonymously.
