Title: "IF YOU PAY RENT, YOU PAY PROPERTY TAX": BRADFORD BRINGS HIS TAX FREEZE TO A YOUNG RENTING FAMILY IN NORTH YORK
Published: 2026-09-23
Issue category: Cost of Living

One day after committing to a one-year property tax freeze at the Board of Trade debate, Bradford says the Mayor doesn't want to run on her record because, for her, this is as good as it gets

TORONTO, ON | This morning in North York, Brad Bradford visited Win and Diana, a young couple who rent their home and are raising their 7-month-old son, Elio, to talk about the one-year property tax freeze he announced at last night's Toronto Region Board of Trade mayoral debate.

Property tax is usually talked about as a homeowner's problem. It isn't. Win and Diana’s landlord pays the property tax on the house they rent, and that cost is built into their rent every month. When City Hall raises property taxes, renters pay for it. They just never see it as a line on the bill.

In 2023, Olivia Chow promised property tax increases would be "modest." Then she delivered a 9.5 per cent increase, then a 6.9 per cent increase, then an election-year 2.2 per cent increase she only got that low by drawing $1.7 billion from the city's reserves. That's nearly 20 per cent compounded, almost $700 more a year on the average home. Renters saw none of the relief.

It happened while the province sent Toronto more help than ever before.

The 2023 New Deal is worth roughly $9 billion to the city over 10 years, including taking the Gardiner Expressway and the Don Valley Parkway off the city's books. More money than ever, higher taxes than ever, and a city that works worse than ever. Food bank visits hit a record 4.1 million last year, up 340 per cent since 2019. Where did the money go?

The Mayor doesn't want to talk about it. At last night's debate, when Bradford talked about the challenges families are facing, the Mayor was upset that he raised them at all. Of course she was. She doesn't want to run on her record. At the Daily Bread debate, she raised her paddle and said Toronto is living up to its full potential. In her view, this is as good as it gets.

"The Mayor doesn't want to run on her record, and I understand why," said Bradford. "She thinks this is as good as it gets. I think Win and Diana deserve better than that. They're doing everything right. They work hard, they pay their bills, and they're trying to build a life here for Elio. Olivia Chow has raised property taxes nearly 20 per cent, and renters have paid their share of every dollar of it. I won't ask this family, or any family, for another dollar until I can show them where the last one went."

The freeze is already paid for. A 2 per cent property tax increase raises about $100 million, and City Hall already has that room. It has it every year. In 2025, for the part of the budget that property taxes pay for, the city budgeted about $5.6 billion and spent about $5.5 billion, $100 million less than it planned. In 2024, that gap was $130 million. Bradford's fiscal plan budgets for what the city actually spends and uses that recurring room to pay for the freeze. That means no property tax increase and no cuts to the front-line services people depend on.

The freeze covers the City Tax Rate on every property in the city, homes and businesses alike. During the freeze, an independent group of experts and former city leaders will carry out the first line by line review of city spending since 2011, covering every division, agency and program. The goal is to protect and improve front-line services while finding the waste, the duplication and the outdated programs. Once the review is done, any future property tax increase will be kept below the rate of inflation.

Now the Mayor is promising to hold increases to "around" the rate of inflation. She promised "modest" last time too. Modest to her is 20%.

"Toronto isn't broken. It's stuck. And stuck is a choice," said Bradford. "The Mayor is offering four more years of the same. I'm offering change. On October 26, Torontonians can choose a city that's safe, a city you can afford, a city that moves. A city where a young family like Win, Diana and Elio can still build a life. A Toronto we can be proud of."
