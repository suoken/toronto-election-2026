Olivia Chow to Break Ground on 10,000 New Rental Homes Next Year

Chow commits to 65,000 homes by 2030 so working families can afford to live in Toronto

TORONTO — Olivia Chow will build another 10,000 rental homes through her Rental Housing Supply Program, including affordable homes, on top of those already in the pipeline. Shovels will hit the ground next year.

With Toronto’s skyline behind her, Chow said the people who work in Toronto should be able to afford to live here. Her commitment builds on housing programs already delivering more than 15,000 homes under construction and sets a goal of 65,000 homes by 2030 – and supporting nearly 40,000 jobs.

“If you’re a nurse, a teacher or a construction worker, you should be able to afford to live here. And not in a shoebox – but in a real home,” said Chow. “These projects are ready. I’ll bring them to the first meeting of Council for approval. No waiting around. Because families cannot put their lives on hold.”

Chow’s rental incentives lower the cost of building homes in exchange for affordable housing. It’s also fast: a project must be able to start construction in the next 12 months to qualify. When the first round opened in November 2024, shovels went into the ground just three months later. The next round of 10,000 homes will only go forward if Chow is re-elected.

Chow has also worked with Prime Minister Mark Carney and Premier Doug Ford to secure funding that helps Toronto build housing while maintaining investment in transit, water and roads.

Chow worked with both governments to secure $1.5 billion for Toronto through the Canada-Ontario Partnership to Build. Toronto was the first municipality to receive a funding announcement under the Development Charge Reduction Program. Chow is leveraging this funding to deliver 10,000 more homes through her signature Rental Housing Supply Program.

That partnership provides greater financial certainty to keep rental housing moving during a slowdown. It also supports larger development-charge reductions for homes with two or more bedrooms, encouraging the family-sized housing Toronto needs.
