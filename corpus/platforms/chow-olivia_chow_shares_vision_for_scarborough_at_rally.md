TORONTO – Olivia Chow held a rally in Scarborough this afternoon with 500 people in attendance, kicking off the final 30 days of the election with supporters from across the city. The afternoon included live music, speakers, and a rally speech celebrating the progress being delivered for Scarborough families, from new community centres and transit to school food programs and affordable homes.

The event was hosted by:

-

Sunthar V, internationally touring comedian and writer from Scarborough

And featured performances by:

-

Madeleine Kay and Brendan Roger of Strings from Paris

-

Salangai Naatiyalaya Kalaikoodam, Tamil dance group

-

DJ MixMangat

And remarks by:

-

Dr. Joseph Wong, Founder, Yee Hong Centre for Geriatric Care

-

Shankari Srithayakumar, Tamil Community Organizer

-

Asma Musa, Coordinator of the Black School Food Working Group

-

Janet Morgan, a personal support worker who crisscrosses Toronto daily on the TTC

Olivia Chow’s Scarborough Rally Speech

Scarborough – are you ready to choose hope over fear? Unity over division? To move forward not backward? Are you ready to win?

I love our city! I love the vibrancy and energy of our neighbourhoods. The street festivals, the food, the music, the people who have come here from every corner of the world.

And Scarborough brings all of that together.

From the bluffs to Rouge Park. From the shops and restaurants along Lawrence Avenue to the families gathering in our parks and community centres. So many cultures. So many languages. So much great food! Part of one city we all call home.

When I came to Toronto as an immigrant kid, I was looking for a place to belong. A lot of you know what I mean! I learned to skate at Nathan Phillips Square. It made me feel like a Canadian. And I loved that it was free. I learned to ride a bike on Centre Island. I spent hours at the Reference Library, using old art books to build my career as a struggling artist.

Our city made me who I am today. An immigrant kid from St. Jamestown who became your mayor. And all of my best memories happened in public spaces. Places we created together.

Now let’s be frank, for way too long Scarborough lacked public spaces. Austerity budgets cut the services your families need to get ahead. Not enough libraries. Not enough TTC service. Not enough community centres. But all of that is changing. We are turning the corner.

Scarborough, we are building the places families deserve! I just opened the beautiful new Rouge Valley Community Centre. Have you seen it? And that new indoor cricket pitch? It’s awesome. Kidstown is open again, with a huge new water play area. We need to move ahead with the Milliken Community Centre expansion. And to everyone who has worked so hard, and dreamed so long, for a Tamil Community Centre: together, we are getting it done!

Thank you Councillor Shan, thank you Tamil community, because this is more than a place for the Tamil community to preserve its culture and heritage. It is a gift to all of Scarborough. And for 30 years, people in Scarborough have also dreamed of an Asian Garden. Now the shovels are hitting the ground this fall right here at the Chinese Cultural Centre!

And we’re not stopping there. Because the people of Scarborough should not have to wait two hours to get across Scarborough! You deserve that time with your families. In just a few days, the new Scarborough Busway opens. That could give a TTC rider an hour back every week!

And we are putting $18 million into design work for the Scarborough East LRT. Scarborough has waited long enough. Let’s build it!

But what good is better transit if families can’t afford to ride it? Well, we’re changing that, too! We’ve frozen TTC fares for three years. This month, rides become free after 47 trips in a month.

And in my next term? Ten rides a week, then free! More time at home. More money in your pocket. That’s what we’re fighting for!

And when Scarborough fights, Scarborough wins! Just yesterday, VIA Rail backed off from cutting Guildwood Station. And we’ll keep fighting to make sure they don’t even try it again.

And we’re fighting for your kids, too. Thirty-five years ago, Jack Layton and I dreamed of feeding every child in every Toronto school. We worked out the cost. We made a plan. We kept at it.

And now, as your mayor, that dream has become a reality! Every classroom! Every school! Every child! Including every child right here in Scarborough!

And yes, we’re building homes. Homes you can afford. Homes for seniors on Sewells Road. New co-op housing at Kennedy Commons. And we are going to keep building, because every family deserves a place to call home!

Things are getting better here in Scarborough. New places to play. Better transit on the way.

Food for our kids. Homes being built. And here’s another thing.

Scarborough is becoming the newest artistic engine of our city. So we need more ways to gather, create, practice and perform. The talent is here, we will support it. We have so much more to do. And we will do it, together!

As a city, we are finally, after a long time, feeling more optimistic. We have momentum.

And now, we have to keep that momentum going. Because these are not easy times. Trump and his tariffs create uncertainty and stress. Our world is rapidly changing around us. Everything is less affordable, for almost everyone. Torontonians are doing their very best, working harder than ever before. Sometimes multiple jobs. And our city needs to support them if Toronto is going to live up to its full potential.

So we have a choice to make. We can choose to work together, support each other, continue building the city we love, a joyous city that invites us and welcomes us. Where we care for each other and stand up for each other. Where we celebrate who we are: ambitious, hardworking, ready to win, ready to go forward. Where we build more housing and lower the cost of living. Where we decide our own future. As Torontonians.

Or we can go backwards. We could choose leaders who will starve our public spaces, cut and burn and slash our services like the bad old days. Divide us instead of bringing us together. We’ve been there. Done that. We are not going backwards. That is not what I want. I do not believe it’s what you want either.

We want a stronger Scarborough. A better Scarborough. Because Scarborough is one of the most vibrant parts of a vibrant city. One of the most diverse parts of a diverse city. And in the future, one of the strongest parts of a stronger city!

So let’s keep building. Together. Let’s keep moving forward. Together. Let’s keep building the Scarborough that Scarborough has always deserved. Where every child has a chance.

Where every family has a place to call home. Where life is more affordable for seniors and families. Where we celebrate who we are and make sure everyone has a place to belong.

Scarborough has helped make Toronto the city we love. We can, and we will, keep building a Toronto that is more caring, affordable and safer. Together. Thank you, Scarborough!
