Title: THE PROVINCE SENT MORE MONEY THAN EVER THEN CHOW RAISED TAXES 20%. WHERE DID THE MONEY GO?
Published: 2026-09-20
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

Bradford sends the media on a City Hall scavenger hunt for the missing money and commits to the first line by line review of city spending since 2011

TORONTO, ON | This morning outside Toronto City Hall, Brad Bradford handed reporters a scavenger hunt. The rules are simple. Somewhere inside that building is more money than any provincial government has ever sent to this city. Go find it.

Under the New Deal signed in 2023, the province committed roughly $9 billion to Toronto over 10 years. It took the Gardiner Expressway and the Don Valley Parkway off the city’s books, saving billions in capital costs, and layered on more than $1.2 billion in new operating funding. No province has ever done more for this city.

Olivia Chow took all of that money and raised your taxes anyway. In 2023 she promised property tax increases would be “modest.” Then she delivered 9.5 per cent, then 6.9 per cent, then an election-year 2.2 per cent bought with $1.7 billion drained from the city’s reserves. That’s nearly 20 per cent compounded, almost $700 more a year on the average home.

And what did Torontonians get for it? A record 4.1 million food bank visits last year, up 340 per cent since 2019, with more than 1 in 10 residents now relying on one to get through the month. The worst traffic congestion in North America. A Union Station no world city would accept as its front door. A downtown square people avoid after dark.

At the Daily Bread Food Bank debate, candidates were handed paddles and asked a simple yes or no question: is Toronto living up to its full potential? Olivia Chow raised her sign and said yes. Bradford rejects that. You don’t produce numbers like these at full potential. We’re not even close. And the money to do better already came through the door.

“The province has never sent more money to Toronto. You have never paid higher taxes. And the city has never worked worse,” said Bradford. “Every family balancing a budget at the kitchen table is asking the same question: where did the money go? I work in that building and I’ve been asking it too. Nobody can give you an answer. That should worry every single taxpayer in this city.”

The scavenger hunt offers the media a few places to start:

-
Find the $11 million the city spent on software licenses for people who no longer work there.

-
Find the $5 million spent on stickers for recycling bins.

-
Find the $142,000 in advertising the Mayor spent telling you how great her 2026 budget was.

-
Find the up to $800,000 City Hall committed to renaming Yonge-Dundas Square while people tell pollsters they don’t feel safe standing in it.

-
Find the 2 island ferries whose price nearly quadrupled to $92 million and which still aren’t in the water.

-
Find the 831 per cent increase in expenses by the CEO of Toronto Community Housing, at an agency with a $4.5 billion repair backlog.

-
Find the $134 million a year the city now spends on overtime, up 18.5 per cent, including a 50 per cent jump at Parks and Recreation right after it grew its full-time staff by 10 per cent.

“Toronto isn’t struggling because you don’t pay enough,” said Bradford. “City Hall is struggling because nobody is minding the books. When you get more money than ever and deliver less than ever, the problem isn’t revenue. The problem is management.”

Bradford committed today that as mayor he will order a full line by line review of every city division, agency and program, the first since 2011.

“Toronto isn’t broken. It’s stuck,” said Bradford. “The money is there. What’s missing is a mayor willing to look for it. On October 26, Torontonians can choose change: a city that’s safe, a city you can afford, a city that moves. A Toronto we can be proud of.”
