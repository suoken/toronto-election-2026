Title: Brad Bradford outlines his mayoral campaign platform
Published: 2026-10-18
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

20 October 2026

A city that's safe, a city you can afford, a city that moves. My platform focuses on crime, congestion and cost.

Eight commitments, each with what it costs and how it's paid for: a publicly owned water utility and a 25% cut to water bills; a one-year property tax freeze; no municipal land transfer tax on the average home; 1,000 more frontline police officers; a TTC money-back guarantee; a seven-point plan to get Toronto moving; 15,000 youth jobs; and budget rules that cap debt. The full costing is in "How we pay for all of this".

Public water and lower bills

I won't sell Toronto Water. It stays 100% publicly owned, the way the City has owned Toronto Hydro since 1999. I'll cut your water bill by 25% on the first $1,500 in annual charges. No form to fill out, no means test, no waiting.

Cost: nothing to taxpayers. In 2024 the system cost $909M to run and billed $1.32B. The cut ends that over-collection. Saving: about $300 a year for the average household.[1, 7]

A one-year property tax freeze

I'll freeze the City property tax rate for homes and businesses for one year while independent experts review spending line by line.

Cost: about $100M. Paid for by: money the City budgets and doesn't spend: about $100M in 2025 and $130M in 2024.[4, 7]

Less tax when you buy a home

I'll eliminate the municipal land transfer tax on a principal residence up to the previous year's average Toronto price, $1.1M for 2025, for first-time buyers and for Toronto homeowners selling one home to buy another. Value above the threshold stays taxed. Investors and corporations keep paying in full.

Saving: about $18,000 on a home at the threshold. Cost: about $300M a year. Paid for by: the $256M (2027) and $369M (2028) the City's own 2026 Budget already moves from operating into capital, financed over the life of the assets instead, plus a dividend from the water utility.[3, 7]

1,000 more frontline officers

I'll add 1,000 frontline officers over my first term, with training expanded through a proposed Ontario Police College satellite campus in Etobicoke.

Cost: about $36M in year one, rising to about $143M a year at full strength in year four, inside a police budget of $1.43B. Paid for: through the annual budget process.[2, 7]

A TTC money-back guarantee

If your trip is delayed 15 minutes or more, you get a refund or credit, with exceptions for events outside the TTC's control, as GO Transit has offered since 2012.

Cost: $3.5M to $7M a year, under 1% of fare revenue.[7]

Transit that works when you need it

A seven-point plan: signal priority for streetcars across the network, published TTC and Metrolinx service plans 30 days ahead of major events, and subway service from 6 a.m. on marathon day.

Cost: set out item by item in the October 18 release.[6]

15,000 youth jobs

I'll challenge employers to create 15,000 permanent, full-time youth jobs by the end of my term, 5,000 of them in year one. Borrowell has signed on as the first Anchor Partner.

Cost: employers fund the jobs; the City coordinates the program for under $750,000 a year.[5]

Budget rules

Borrowing is for capital only. Debt interest never exceeds 4 cents per dollar of revenue, lower than any province. Total debt costs never exceed 10% of own-source revenue. The operating budget balances every year, as the Municipal Act requires.[7]
