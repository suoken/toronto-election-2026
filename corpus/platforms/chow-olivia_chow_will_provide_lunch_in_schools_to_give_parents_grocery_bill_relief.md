Olivia Chow Will Provide Lunch in Schools to Give Parents Grocery Bill Relief

Families could save up to $1,800 per child every year, says Chow.

TORONTO - Olivia Chow will provide every child lunch in school, saving families $1,800 per year on groceries and saving parents time making lunch every day. As mayor, Chow expanded the school food program into every school, providing at least a mid-morning meal to 330,000 students across Toronto. If re-elected, she will go further and deliver a full lunch, using the city’s bulk buying power to reduce grocery costs for families.

“Prices at big grocery stores just keep rising. With a robust school food program, we can give parents some breathing room. We can save families thousands of dollars a year feeding kids in school. And give parents a break on making lunch every day," said Chow. “Back in 1990, Jack Layton and I made a plan to feed every kid in Toronto schools. More than ever, school food can save families money and help kids focus on their teacher, not their hunger. We need to keep moving forward, not backwards.”

Chow’s school lunch program could save families over $1,800 per child annually. The expansion to lunch will be phased in. The program expansion is funded like the existing school food program, which is jointly by the City, other levels of government, and private donations.

Background:

-

As mayor, Olivia Chow expanded the school food program to all Toronto schools, serving 330,000 students. She also invested in building the infrastructure to deliver the program and created local jobs in the logistics (food preparation, delivery) of the program.

-

Studies show that when fully implemented, universal school meals (morning meal and lunch) could save families up to $1800 per year.

-

See the First letter from Councillor Jack Layton on establishing a school food program in 1991.
