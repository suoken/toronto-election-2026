Title: BRADFORD ANNOUNCES PLAN TO EXPAND POLICE TRAINING SO TORONTO CAN TRAIN ITS OWN POLICE OFFICERS AGAIN
Published: 2026-10-17
Issue category: Crime

Brad Bradford announced he will work with the province to open a satellite campus of the Ontario Police College in Toronto, so the city can train the 1,000 new front line officers he has committed to hire by the end of his first year in office

TORONTO, ON | Today Brad Bradford announced that a Bradford administration will work with the Province of Ontario to open a satellite campus of the Ontario Police College in Etobicoke, running additional recruit classes to train the officers Toronto needs.

Bradford has committed to hiring 1,000 net new sworn Toronto police officers by the end of his first term in office. Every one of them has to be trained first. Today, nearly every recruit in the province goes through the Ontario Police College in Aylmer, and Toronto shares those seats with every other police service in Ontario.

"NDP Mayor Chow says the city can't train more officers. The training pipeline reflects her ambition, not Toronto's limit. Change the ambition and you change the pipeline," said Bradford. "Where there's a will, there's a way. We're not putting a man on the moon. We're putting police officers on the streets."

To deliver the officers, a Bradford government will work with the province to pull every lever:

-
Open a satellite campus of the Ontario Police College at the Toronto Police College in Etobicoke, running additional recruit classes for Toronto officers on top of the existing provincial intake.

-
Secure more seats dedicated to Toronto recruits in Ontario Police College classes.

-
Improve retention of the experienced officers the Service already has, so every new hire adds to the front line instead of replacing someone walking out the door.

This has happened before. When the city urgently needed officers, Ontario Police College training was delivered at C.O. Bick College in Scarborough. More recently, the Ontario Police College has run larger recruit classes while upholding its professional standards, and opened a satellite campus in Cornwall to train additional recruits. If Cornwall can host a police college campus, the largest city in the country can.

Toronto has grown 21% since 2010, about half a million more people, and has roughly the same number of deployed officers it had then. The Toronto Police Service's own five year hiring plan, approved by the Police Services Board in December 2024, adds about 502 net new officers by 2029 and reaches 172 officers per 100,000 residents. Montreal is at 221. Vancouver is at 196. Edmonton is at 188. Winnipeg is at 186. The current plan adds only about 250 net new officers during the next term of Council. Bradford's 1,000 are front line officers: on patrol, in neighbourhoods and at subway stations, not behind desks.

How we pay for it: The Toronto Police Service 2026 budget sets aside $20.5 million to hire 143 new officers, about $143,000 per officer fully loaded. Phased in over a first term, the commitment will rise to about $143 million a year at full strength in year 4. That is about 10% of today's $1.43 billion police budget, reached over four years, inside a budget that already grows roughly 7% a year and rose $94 million in 2026 alone. The cost is not in dispute. The question is whether a city that has added half a million people should pay it. Bradford says yes. Safety and affordability are not a trade-off.

The province has never given the City of Toronto more money than it has over the past three years. NDP Mayor Chow still raised property taxes nearly 20%, the largest increase in the city's history. If she's re-elected she'll raise them another 20% at least. Where did the money go?

NDP Mayor Chow stood on the debate stage and declared that Toronto has reached its full potential. Bradford rejects that. A city with half a million more people and no more police on the street is not at its full potential. We're not even close.

"A city that is not safe is not free," said Bradford. "Toronto isn't broken. It's stuck. We're on the cusp of change. Change is within our grasp. But to change the city, we need to change the mayor."
