Title: BRADFORD: TORONTO’S BEST DAYS ARE AHEAD OF US, IF WE CHOOSE THEM
Published: 2026-10-08
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

TORONTO, ON | Today at the CBC mayoral debate, Brad Bradford made the case for change. A city that is safe. A city that moves. A city families can afford to stay in. And a City Hall that is modernized, accountable, and treats every dollar residents send it with the respect it deserves.

“Toronto isn’t broken. It’s stuck,” said Bradford after the debate. “Residents give this city their time, their talent and their taxes, and every year they pay more and get less. I’m running because I believe Toronto’s best days are ahead of us. We’re on the cusp of change. Change is within our grasp. But to change the city, we need to change the mayor.”

The province has never given the City of Toronto more money than it has over the past 3 years. NDP Mayor Chow still raised property taxes nearly 20%, the largest increase in the city’s history. If she’s re-elected she’ll raise them another 20% at least. In the same 3 years, food bank visits climbed to 4.1 million, up 340% since 2019. The money came in, the taxes went up, and families are lining up for groceries. Where did the money go?

Bradford’s plan starts with affordability. A property tax freeze in year 1, for homes and businesses. Water bills cut 25%, about $300 a year for the average household. The land transfer tax eliminated on homes below the $1.1 million average price, the biggest opportunity to buy a home this city has ever offered, by saving about $18,000. And the first full, independent, line by line review of City spending since 2011, so that every dollar is accounted for before anyone at City Hall asks residents for another one.

“Respecting your tax dollars doesn’t mean doing less for people. It means doing things properly,” continued Bradford. “Ontario modernized its budget 20 years ago. Ottawa did it 30 years ago. City Hall still runs on a system that lets $1.6 billion holes open up and nobody can tell you where the money went. I’m a city planner by training. I know how cities are supposed to work, and I know this one can work a lot better than it does. That’s not a cut. That’s a modern city government that earns the taxes it collects.”

A more affordable city has to be a safer city. As Mayor, Bradford will take his seat on the Toronto Police Services Board, put 1,000 more front line officers on the street and a visible police presence at every subway station, and go after the repeat violent offenders who are terrorizing the same neighbourhoods over and over. And it has to be a city that moves: a 15 minute service guarantee on the TTC, and a single point of accountability for construction, with published timelines and consequences when they slip.

“A city that is not safe is not free,” said Bradford. “And a city where you spend 2 hours a day in traffic that nobody can explain is a city taxing your time on top of your money. Nobody voted for that. These aren’t separate problems. Crime, congestion and cost are the 3 things standing between families and the life they came to this city to build. Fix them, and Toronto takes off.”

NDP Mayor Chow spent today's debate trying to pin things on Brad Bradford that he had nothing to do with. Bradford spent it talking about Toronto. The Mayor held him responsible for things that happened before he was first elected in 2018, and for decisions made at Queen’s Park. It’s the same Mayor who stood on the debate stage and declared that Toronto has reached its full potential, in a city with 4.1 million food bank visits a year. Bradford rejects that. We’re not even close.

“The Mayor will likely spend the next 18 days trying to scare people, with facts that aren't true. When you’ve made your peace with less, the only argument you have left is that change isn't necessary,” said Bradford. “I’m going to spend them talking about what this city can be. The best city in the world to raise a family, to build a business, and to reach your full potential. Not someday. Now. That’s the choice on October 26, and I’m asking everyone who knows Toronto can do better to make it.”
