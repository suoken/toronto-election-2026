Olivia Chow will Create More Local Jobs for Scarborough Youth

Chow will guarantee local jobs building the Scarborough East LRT

SCARBOROUGH — Olivia Chow announced she will create jobs for Scarborough youth by ensuring local workers are hired to build the Scarborough East LRT, invest in community-designed youth safety and mentorship programs, and create 15,000 new youth jobs across the city.

She made the announcement joining young people at YouthLink, a community agency that supports youth and families in Scarborough.

“We need to make sure young people in Scarborough can access good jobs. The City is building new infrastructure, through that we can create opportunities to hire local young people and workers. Now that we’re building the Scarborough East LRT, the young people who live here should be the ones getting the jobs,” said Chow. “A good first job, a paid apprenticeship, a path to a real career: that’s how we give young people a future in their own neighbourhood. That’s how we build a safer, more affordable Scarborough.”

Olivia Chow will:

-

Create more youth jobs for Scarborough: By using Community Benefits Agreements, guaranteeing that 20% of construction jobs on the Scarborough East LRT project go to local residents, with a focus on youth - and a target 15% of apprentices on the project for local youth aged 18 to 35. This is double the local hiring commitments made on the Finch West and Eglinton Crosstown LRTs.

-

Invest in Scarborough youth safety and mentorship: Convene community leaders and young people through her Mayor’s Youth Safety Task Force and invest $28 million in violence prevention programs, mentorship and jobs.

-

Create 15,000 New Youth Jobs through the Toronto Youth Employment Strategy, in partnership with the United Way. This partnership has already created 2,600 jobs in a few months.

Under Chow’s leadership, the City of Toronto is one of the city’s largest youth employers with over 16,000 jobs. Just recently, Chow launched the Toronto Youth Employment Strategy, partnering with the United Way Greater Toronto, and created 2,600 jobs in just the first 6 months. See here.

BACKGROUND:

Community Benefits Agreements for local jobs the Scarborough East LRT:

Community Benefits Agreements (CBAs) are legally binding agreements attached to major construction, real estate, or public infrastructure projects. guarantee specific social and economic benefits for local and marginalized communities. The City of Toronto Community Benefits Framework guides how the city integrates community benefits into its own purchasing, leasing, and major developments.

Notable local examples include:

-

Rexdale – Casino (2018) Woodbine Community Benefits Agreement

-

40% of hire for local and equity-seeking groups for the casino

-

10% of construction jobs as local and equity seeking hires

-

Eglinton Crosstown LRT (2014)

-

10% aspirational target of all trades and/craft working hours on a trade-by-trade basis to be performed by apprentices from historically disadvantaged community and/or equity seeking groups
