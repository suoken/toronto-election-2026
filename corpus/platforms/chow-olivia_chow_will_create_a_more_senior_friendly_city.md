Olivia Chow will create a more senior-friendly city

Chow’s plan helps seniors age at home and provides more social connection

TORONTO — Olivia Chow will make it easier for seniors to age at home and stay social.

“People want to be able to stay in their homes, in communities they love, as they age. We can help make life easier so that's possible. Maybe you need help around the house with cleaning. Or you want to get together with your neighbors for a book club or walking group. We can make Toronto a more senior-friendly city,” said Chow.

Chow’s plan will reach 28,000 seniors with more support at home, more mobile clinics and more access to recreation. Physical and social wellbeing so you can stay in your home longer.

-

More homemaking services and health clinics near home. 5,000 seniors will be able to access Toronto’s free homemaking services, which helps with cleaning, laundry, meal prep, and household basics. By expanding mobile dental and primary care clinics, we can help 8,000 seniors stay healthy near home.

-

More recreation and social connection by partnering with UHN to hire Community Connectors that establish recreation and social opportunities in areas with a lot of seniors - called naturally occurring retirement communities (NORCs). With this plan, we can reach 15,000 seniors in 100 NORCs.

As Mayor, Olivia Chow delivered more affordability and improved access to services for seniors. She invested in free seniors library programming, created and expanded the free air conditioner program and invested more in homemaker services. She expanded the property tax deferral and cancellation programs, increasing the threshold each year, and will continue to do so if re-elected.

Toronto’s population is rapidly aging. A fifth of Toronto residents will be over 65 by 2041. Most seniors want to stay in their homes and access the services they need in the neighbourhoods and communities they know and love.
