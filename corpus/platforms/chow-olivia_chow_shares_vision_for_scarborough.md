Olivia Chow shares vision for Scarborough

Better transit, creating local jobs and building safer neighbourhoods

SCARBOROUGH — Surrounded by families at the Scarborough Civic Library, Olivia Chow shared her plan to keep Scarborough moving forward: easing congestion, creating local jobs and investing in safer neighbourhoods.

“My mother was a hotel maid. She was an immigrant who wanted what every parent wants. A decent home. Her bills to be paid. A better life for her child,” said Chow. “So many people here in Scarborough understand my story. Because it’s your story, too.”

“Scarborough is the engine of Toronto. Full of hard working, resourceful, creative people who contribute so much to our city. For too long, Scarborough was told to wait. Wait for better transit. Wait for investment. Wait for your turn,” said Chow. “Well, not anymore. Scarborough’s turn is right now!”

Chow shared her vision for a Scarborough that’s more connected, with more opportunity and jobs, and that’s safe for everyone:

Getting Scarborough moving:

-

Prioritize Scarborough for new smart traffic lights and intelligent intersections that respond to real-time traffic conditions.

-

Build the Scarborough East LRT and continue increasing TTC service.

-

Introduce weekly fare capping: pay for ten rides, then ride free for the rest of the week.

-

Make the Scarborough Busway permanent and remove the temporary red bus lanes on Kennedy and Midland.

Creating local jobs, especially for youth:

-

Guaranteeing that 20% jobs building the Scarborough East LRT go to local residents, and target 15% of apprenticeships for youth aged 18 to 35.

-

Create 15,000 new youth jobs through the Toronto Youth Employment Strategy.

-

Keep building on recent Scarborough investments - for example Hitachi’s 1,200 jobs and Toyota’s 1,000 jobs - by selling Scarborough as a destination for investment.

Making neighbourhoods safer

-

Support more Scarborough youth through violence prevention programs, mentorship, recreation and jobs.

-

Hire more police officers through the multi-year hiring plan, which hires 360 officers per year until 2029.

“Every crane means jobs. Workers buying lunch. Shopping at local grocery stores. Supporting Scarborough businesses,” said Chow. “Let’s build the Scarborough that people always deserved.”

Chow also urged Scarborough to vote on October 26.

“This election is a simple choice. Do we choose to keep moving forward together to make life more affordable? Or do we go backwards with cuts to the TTC and libraries?” said Chow. “I will keep standing up for families here in Scarborough and all across our city.”
