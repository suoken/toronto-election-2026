People ask fair questions about eight years on council. Here are the answers, with the dates, the votes and the documents, so you can check every one.

Did he vote for the Bloor bike lanes?

Yes. On June 14, 2023, he voted for the Bloor Street West extension from Runnymede Road to Resurrection Road (2023.IE4.3). On August 11, 2026, he said he "got this one wrong", citing traffic and the effect on local businesses. His plan would remove the lanes from South Kingsway west to Resurrection Road and put cycling investment on residential streets and multi-use trails.

Campaign release, August 11, 2026 https://bradford26.ca/news/etobicoke-bike-lanes

Did he vote to rename Yonge-Dundas Square?

Yes. On July 14, 2021, he supported beginning the Dundas Street and civic-asset renaming process, which passed 17 to 7 (2021.EX25.1). On December 14, 2023, he supported renaming the square and the Jane/Dundas Library, each passed 19 to 2, and opposed renaming the two subway stations (2023.MM13.29). Council also paused the remaining street renaming. On June 29, 2026, his campaign proposed renaming the square Toronto Square, a name every Torontonian can share.

Did he vote to cut the police budget?

Yes. On June 29, 2020, he supported Parts 2 and 3 of Councillor Matlow's motion 1a, asking for a 2021 police budget at least 10 per cent below the approved 2020 budget, with the money reinvested in communities. Those parts lost 8 to 16 (2020.CC22.2). The same day he supported body-worn cameras, which passed. His October 17, 2026 release proposes 1,000 net new officers over a first term, about $143 million a year at full strength.

What were his 2022 council and 2023 mayoral election results?

On October 24, 2022, he was re-elected in Ward 19, Beaches–East York, with 15,169 votes, 54.7 per cent of the vote; the runner-up received 6,291. In the June 26, 2023 mayoral by-election he finished eighth citywide with 9,254 votes, and Olivia Chow won with 269,372.
