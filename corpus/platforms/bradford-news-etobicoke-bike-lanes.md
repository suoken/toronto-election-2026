Title: BRADFORD WILL REMOVE ETOBICOKE BLOOR ST. W BIKE LANES  AND BUILD A CITYWIDE NETWORK FOR CYCLISTS
Published: 2026-08-11
Issue category: Congestion

Bike lanes belong on residential side streets and in parks, hydro corridors and ravines, not squeezed onto Etobicoke's main street

TORONTO, ON | As part of his plan to alleviate congestion in Toronto, Brad Bradford committed that as Mayor he will remove the bike lanes on Bloor Street West from South Kingsway west to Resurrection Road, restore the traffic lanes, and direct city staff to begin the work in his first year. The removal is paired with a plan to redirect the city's cycling investment into a network of residential streets and multi-use trails in parks, hydro corridors and ravines.

“People here in Etobicoke told City Hall exactly what these lanes would do to their main street, and City Hall didn't listen,” said Bradford. “Commuters in Etobicoke have seen with their own eyes, every day for two years, the congestion and chaos that they have created. Within my first year in office as your Mayor, these lanes are coming out, the traffic lanes are going back in, and that is my commitment. Not another review.”

The lanes were installed in 2023 and 2024 against overwhelming objections from local residents and businesses. In February 2025, more than 40 local businesses filed a $10 million lawsuit against the City of Toronto over the lanes and the consultation process that produced them.

“City Hall needs to have the humility to acknowledge when something isn’t working, and to go back and fix it. These bike lanes in Etobicoke aren’t working,” said Bradford.

Bradford's plan, Build Bike Lanes Where They Belong, will:

-
Create an integrated network of residential streets and multi-use trails in parks, hydro corridors and ravines that gives cyclists a safer, faster alternative to riding on arterial roads.

-
Properly maintain key trails through the winter so they are safe and usable year round.

-
Complete the trail and connector projects that have languished in municipal plans for fifteen years, including the 81 kilometre Loop Trail and the Meadoway in Scarborough.

-
Finish the West Toronto Railpath at a responsible cost, after the current extension's price tag climbed toward $200 million.

“I'm a city planner and a cyclist. I want more people on bikes in this city, not fewer. I also want less congestion, and for everyone to make it home safely and on time,” said Bradford. “Toronto is sitting on the largest ravine system in the world and hydro corridors that run for kilometres in every direction. And our residential neighbourhoods have quiet, welcoming streets. Build the network there, keep it plowed in the winter, and everybody gets what they actually want. Cyclists ride without fighting trucks for space, drivers get their lanes back, and businesses get their customers back.”

For nearly two years, decisions about Toronto's bike lanes have been fought between City Hall and Queen's Park in the courts, at a cost to taxpayers and with no resolution for the street. The province's Bill 212 ordered the removal of the Bloor, Yonge and University bike lanes in November 2024; the Superior Court ruled the forced removal unconstitutional in July 2025; and the province's appeal was heard by the Court of Appeal in January 2026, with a decision still pending.

“Toronto's streets should be planned by Toronto, with the voices of Toronto residents,” said Bradford. “When the city gets this right itself, Queen's Park doesn't need to reach into our streets at all.”

Bradford will pursue federal partnership for the trail network, including active transportation and climate adaptation funding programs.
