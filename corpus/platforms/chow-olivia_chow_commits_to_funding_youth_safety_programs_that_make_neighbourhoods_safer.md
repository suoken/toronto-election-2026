TORONTO — Olivia Chow is committing to fund youth safety and violence prevention programs to make neighbourhoods safer and give young people more hope.

She made the announcement at Malvern Family Resource Centre in Scarborough, standing alongside mothers who lost their sons to gun violence. Speakers included 17-year-old Ocean, who received local work experience through Chow’s CityServe program, and Shondelle Reis, who works to empower young people through her organization JBR Family Pursuing Change.

“We’ve proven that investing in young people works, and I’m not going to stop now,” said Chow. “Every mentorship, every job, every safe place to go after school builds the Toronto we want, a city where every young person believes there’s a future worth reaching for. That’s a safer city for everyone. That’s the city I’m going to keep building.”

Each year as Mayor, Olivia Chow has been investing in addressing youth violence through her Summer Safety Plan and year-round initiatives. In 2025, youth violence was down 40% due to these investments.

She is committing to continuing her Youth Violence Prevention Program funding and increasing it by 40% to reach more young people:

-

Scaling up violence prevention and intervention initiatives with a proven track record of success, reaching over 15,000 youth and 500 parents and caregivers each year, such as the CityServeTO program.

-

Prevent even younger children and youth (starting from age 6) from heading down the wrong path by reaching them earlier in life with mentorship, recreation and arts programs.

“This election is a choice. We could keep moving forward and funding safety programs for young people, or we can go backwards and let candidates who don’t believe in these successful programs cut them,” said Chow. “I choose to move forward, and build a more caring, more affordable, and safer city, together.

Background

Olivia Chow’s Comprehensive Approach to safety

Today’s announcement comes as part of Olivia Chow’s comprehensive approach to safety:

-

Improving 911 call times by 90%

-

Expanding the Community Crisis Service city-wide

-

Funding hiring plans for police, paramedics and firefighters

-

5-year police hiring plan that will hire 1,800 new officers

-

3-year hiring plan to bring on 362 front-line paramedics, superintendents and support staff

-

Recently completed hiring 156 new firefighters through a 3-year hiring plan

-

Investing in transit safety with more than 420 more frontline workers, including police officers, crisis workers, social services teams, Provincial Offence Officers, Special Constables and more.

Olivia Chow’s approach to youth jobs

-

Under Chow’s leadership, the City of Toronto is one of the city’s largest youth employers with over 16,000 jobs.

-

-
