Title: BRADFORD ANNOUNCES TORONTO YOUTH EMPLOYMENT CHALLENGE
Published: 2026-09-03
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

With the Canada Investment Summit just around the corner, Bradford announces the Toronto Youth Employment Challenge, a City Hall-led drive to mobilize the city's private sector to put young Torontonians to work

TORONTO, ON | Toronto is home to a generation of young, talented and motivated people who are eager to learn, work, build careers and raise families right here in the city. But with youth unemployment in Toronto above 15 per cent by the City's own reporting, and the share of young Canadians holding a job having fallen last year to its lowest level since 1998, City Hall is failing them.

Visiting TAMOGO Canada, a North York-based not-for-profit that provides settlement services and employment support, mayoral candidate Brad Bradford announced the Toronto Youth Employment Challenge: a mayor-led initiative that will mobilize Toronto's private sector and its 1.4 million-strong workforce to get young Torontonians the paycheques they're looking for.

Young people in this city face a record-high cost of living, smothering congestion, scarce housing and now the worst youth job market in a generation, a combination that is driving talent away just as Toronto should be competing for it.

"Toronto must compete with the best cities in the world for talent and jobs. Instead, we're driving young, ambitious talent out of the city. Not because they stopped loving Toronto, but because they stopped believing it's a place where they can find a good job and build a good life," said Bradford.

"The response from Mayor Chow has been the status quo: accepting defeat, and pouring more money into the approach that got us here. I won't accept that, and young people shouldn't either. This is why City Hall needs to change how it works, with real solutions that use every advantage this city has, and the urgency young Torontonians deserve."

Bradford's plan: the Toronto Youth Employment Challenge, in five pillars. As Mayor, Bradford will:

-
Declare a public goal of creating 15,000 permanent full-time jobs for youth on his first day in office, creating accountability and calling on the city's employers to participate.

-
Personally recruit 10 to 15 top CEOs as founding Anchor Partners, who will drive job creation and tap into a wealth of young, ambitious and ready-to-work Torontonians.

-
Build a public pledge registry and portal that puts Toronto youth job postings in one place, with a quarterly leaderboard of jobs pledged and jobs created.

-
Require that at least 30 per cent of internships, apprenticeships and Anchor Partner positions go to equity-deserving youth, in communities where youth unemployment across Canada ran between 23 and 26 per cent last year.

-
Hold employers accountable by publishing outcomes against pledges, with three-year renewal terms and working with delivery and community partners.

The Challenge requires no new taxpayer-funded jobs program. Employers fund the positions; the City funds only the coordination behind them, at under $750,000 a year. Toronto doesn't need to reinvent the wheel. In New York City, the New York Jobs CEO Council has hired more than 65,000 New Yorkers since 2021. Toronto, with the country's largest financial centre and Canada's biggest tech hub, has never mounted anything like it.

"As the Prime Minister and the Government of Canada work to attract jobs and investment to Toronto with the Summit, my commitment as Mayor is that those companies will find a young, ambitious and talented workforce ready to fill those roles, right here," said Bradford. "And my message to the young people of Toronto is don't give up hope. This October, there's a clear choice: a City Hall content with the status quo, or change at City Hall that puts you first, so you can come to Toronto, build a career and grow a family. That's what building a city we can be proud of is all about."

"Toronto isn't broken. It's stuck," added Bradford. "On October 26th, we can get it unstuck, and put this city's young people to work."
