Title: TORONTO'S COMEBACK STARTS NOW’: BRADFORD LAUNCHES 25/7 TOUR OF ALL 25 WARDS IN THE FINAL 7 DAYS OF THE CAMPAIGN
Published: 2026-10-19
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

Watch Brad’s new ad: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Pg_D-q4oGE&feature=youtu.be

TORONTO, ON | Today Brad Bradford launched the final stretch of his campaign for mayor, Toronto's Comeback, built on one belief: this city has not reached its full potential. Not even close. And every comeback starts with someone who refuses to accept that this is as good as it gets.

"Olivia Chow stood on the debate stage and declared that Toronto has reached its full potential. I reject that," said Bradford. "4.1 million food bank visits is not our full potential. The worst commutes in North America is not our full potential. Half a million more people and no more police officers on the street is not our full potential. Toronto can do so much better. And in 7 days, we will."

Over the final week of the campaign, Bradford will take a positive, ambitious plan to every corner of the city. Starting today, The 25/7 Tour will take him to all 25 wards in 7 days, from Etobicoke to Scarborough, from the waterfront to Steeles.

The campaign is also releasing its final ad of the race today, featuring Bradford as a leader and a father with a positive vision for the city and the momentum to deliver it. Watch it here

The plan comes down to the 3 things families feel every single day:

A city that feels safe. People don't feel safe, and the numbers say they're right. Toronto Police data to October 12 shows auto theft up 5.8% this year, 324 more cars stolen than this time last year. Assaults are up. Break and enters are up. Thefts over $5,000 are up 6.1%. Home invasions are on the rise. Bradford's plan: 1,000 net new front line police officers by the end of a first term, with an officer at every subway station, targeted enforcement on repeat violent offenders and a Toronto campus of the Ontario Police College so the city can train its own officers again.

A city that moves. Improving transit safety and reliability, including a 15-minute service guarantee on the TTC, a single point of accountability for construction with published timelines and real consequences, and an end to the time tax. The time tax is the mom who misses bedtime because the streetcar sat in traffic. It's the dad who gets to soccer practice as the whistle blows for the end of the game. It's the hours nobody voted for and nobody gets back.

A city you can afford. A property tax freeze in year one for homes and businesses. Water bills cut 25%, about $300 a year for the average household. The land transfer tax eliminated on homes below $1.1 million, about $18,000 back on closing day. And the first full, independent, line by line review of City Hall spending since 2011. These are the levers the city actually controls, and a Bradford administration will pull every one of them.

Underneath all of it is a commitment to modernize the City's budget: a true separation of operating and capital spending, the way Ontario and Ottawa do it. Families don't sit at the kitchen table talking about capital budgets. But they expect the person running a $16 billion city to think about it, and they take comfort knowing someone capable is at the helm who understands what their family needs.

"I'm a city planner by training. I know how cities are supposed to work, and I know this one can work better," said Bradford. "Safe streets. Less time stuck in traffic. A water bill and a tax bill you can actually plan around. That's not a dream. That's a plan. That's a comeback. And over the next 7 days I'm going to take it to every single ward in this city, because every neighbourhood is part of it."

NDP Mayor Chow has put forward nothing ambitious in this campaign. She came into office with a $1.8 billion hole in the budget and is leaving with a $1.6 billion hole, with no plan to close it. The province has never given the City of Toronto more money than it has over the past 3 years. NDP Mayor Chow still raised property taxes nearly 20%, the largest increase in the city's history. If she's re-elected she'll raise them another 20% at least. Where did the money go?

Rather than defend that record, the Mayor has handed her campaign to Progress Toronto, a third party attack group now running a campaign of fear and misinformation against Brad Bradford through flyers at the door and online.

"When you have no plan, you run on fear. When you have a plan, you run on it. I'm running on mine," said Bradford. "Toronto isn't broken. It's stuck. We're on the cusp of change. Change is within our grasp. But to change the city, we need to change the mayor. Toronto's comeback starts now."
