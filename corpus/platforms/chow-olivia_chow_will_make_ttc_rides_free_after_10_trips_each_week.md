TORONTO — Olivia Chow is introducing a 10 ride a week fare cap. After 10 paid rides, the rest of the week is free. Chow will also freeze TTC fares for a fourth straight year.

The new weekly fare cap means that after you pay for 10 trips, every additional TTC trip that week is free. If you commute to work every weekday and take transit three additional times in a week, for example heading to a Blue Jays game, going shopping, or meeting friends, you could see $19.80 in weekly savings - about $1,000 a year.

“People are working hard and everything costs more. Your city should be in your corner,” said Chow. “If you take the TTC to work every day, your rides in the evenings and on the weekend will be free. It is simple, automatic, and it puts real money back in your pocket.”

Before Chow became mayor, TTC riders had been hit with repeated fare increases and service cuts. She made a different choice: she froze fares, restored service levels and secured a New Deal that put the TTC on stronger financial footing. Chow’s three year fare freeze alone has saved a working couple about $300 in the past year.

Under the new plan, you won’t need to buy a pass or pay a big amount upfront. Just tap the same PRESTO card, debit card or credit card as you travel. Once you reach 10 paid TTC trips in a week, the rest are free.

A weekly cap also means riders can begin saving sooner. If you work from home, get sick or go away for part of a month, you won’t lose the chance to save because you missed a monthly threshold. This will encourage more people to choose transit, helping reduce congestion.

“This election is a choice,” said Chow. “We can keep moving forward, making transit better and lowering costs, or go back to higher fares, service cuts and a city that asks working people to pay more for less. I choose to keep Toronto moving forward.”
