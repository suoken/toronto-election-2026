Title: BRADFORD: "WHEN I'M MAYOR, THE EGLINTON EAST LRT IS MY TOP TRANSIT PRIORITY"
Published: 2026-09-07
Issue category: Congestion

"The people of Scarborough have been overlooked by Mayor Chow for long enough," says Bradford, pledging a personal funding push to Queen's Park and Ottawa in his first 100 days and shovels in the ground by the end of the term

TORONTO, ON | Standing outside the University of Toronto Scarborough Campus this morning, Brad Bradford made his commitment to Scarborough plain: when he's mayor, the Eglinton East LRT is his top transit priority, backed by a funding mission to Queen's Park and Ottawa in his first 100 days and a commitment to get shovels in the ground before the end of his first term.

Every day, hundreds of thousands of Scarborough residents pay the Olivia Chow Time Tax at a rate no other part of Toronto would accept. Students at the University of Toronto Scarborough spend upwards of two hours getting to a campus that should be twenty minutes away. Workers from Malvern heading to a job or a hospital shift ride a system that has quietly trained them to expect less. The Eglinton East LRT is the project that changes that, and it has been waiting more than a decade for a mayor willing to fight for it.

"When I'm mayor, this is my top transit priority," said Bradford. "The people of Scarborough have been overlooked by this mayor for long enough. The plan exists. The route is drawn. The environmental assessment is done. What's missing is a mayor who fights for this line the way City Hall has always fought for downtown. That's the change I'm offering, and I'll start by taking Scarborough's case to Queen's Park and Ottawa myself, in my first 100 days."

The Eglinton East LRT is an 18.6-kilometre line from Kennedy Station through eastern Scarborough to Malvern Town Centre, with 27 stops serving UTSC, Centennial College and eight Neighbourhood Improvement Areas. The city has put roughly $1.2 billion on the table toward the $4.65-billion project. The province has committed nothing, and in April the provincial Transportation Minister said the line is "a municipally-led project" that is not on the province's priority list. The federal government has committed nothing. There is no construction date.

This spring, after Bradford forced the issue at City Council, NDP Mayor Olivia Chow responded. She gave the project a new name, the Scarborough East Rapid Transit line, declared it the city's top transit priority, added $10 million for design work, and struck a working group. Five months later, not one senior government dollar has been committed and no construction date exists. The downtown waterfront LRT, which was advanced by unanimous Council vote in June 2022 as an equal pair with Scarborough's line, received $3 billion in committed three-way funding in March. Scarborough received a rebrand.

"A new name is not a transit line. A working group is not a shovel. Scarborough can't ride a rename," said Bradford. "The downtown waterfront got the Prime Minister, the Premier and three billion dollars. Scarborough got a press release. That's just how it is in Olivia Chow's Toronto, and it doesn't have to be."

At the end of this month, the Mayor will open the Scarborough Busway, conveniently just in time for an election. Bradford supports the busway as what it was always meant to be: a bridge. As mayor, he will make sure it stays a bridge and not a destination, by tying it publicly to a construction start date for the LRT it was built to precede.

As mayor, Bradford will make the Eglinton East LRT Toronto's top transit priority in action, not in name. He will personally lead Toronto's funding case to the provincial and federal governments in his first 100 days, making the ask respectfully, across the table, with a business case that serves eight Neighbourhood Improvement Areas, two post-secondary campuses and tens of thousands of transit-dependent residents. He will get shovels in the ground by 2030, the end of the next Council term. And he will fight for a Kennedy Station connection done right, so riders get a fast, seamless link to the rest of the network.

"Scarborough has been asked to accept less for so long it's started to feel normal. It's not normal," said Bradford. "Toronto isn't broken. It's stuck. And nowhere is it more stuck than on a bus in Scarborough. A stuck city only gets moving one way, and that's change. On October 26th, Scarborough can choose change. Not later. Right now."
