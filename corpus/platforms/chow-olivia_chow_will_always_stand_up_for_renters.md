Olivia Chow will always stand up for renters

Chow joins Beaches-East York residents her opponent has ignored for years

BEACHES-EAST YORK — In the final days of the campaign, Chow again stood with tenants at 500 Dawes, a building that has become a powerful symbol for her work protecting renters and supporting residents who are ignored by their local councillor.

The choice in this election is between Chow, who stands up for people, or her opponent, who has ignored his own ward residents for years. Just ask the tenants at 500 Dawes.

“I grew up in one of Toronto’s older rental buildings. On my mother’s wages as a hotel maid, we survived, but it's harder now for renters. We need to keep working to make it easier to have a stable, affordable and safe home,” said Chow. “Half of Torontonians rent their homes, they deserve a mayor that will be a strong voice for them at City Hall.”

“This election is a simple choice. Do we choose to keep moving forward together to protect renters and make life more affordable? Or do we go backwards with cuts to services under a mayor who ignored people in his own ward for years?” said Chow. “I will keep standing up for families that rent their homes. I always have and always will.”

BACKGROUND

Olivia Chow’s support for renters as mayor:

-

Renoviction Bylaw to stop illegal evictions under the guise of renovations. See here.

-

Doubling RentSafeTO inspectors over the course of her budgets

-

Boosting funding for eviction prevention programs, including the Rent Bank

-

Piloting the City’s powers of Remedial Action at 500 Dawes. See here and here.

-

Free Air Conditioner program for low income seniors and families with small children, about 1,500 provided last summer.

-

Developing a Maximum Temperature By-law that provides renters healthy and cool apartments in the summer, while ensuring it doesn’t raise rents via AGIs

Campaign commitments:

-

Bad Landlords Task Force consisting of enforcement officers and lawyers with a mandate to pursue the worst buildings and landlords, and secure court orders for repairs.

-

Ramping up “remedial action:” For landlords that chronically refuse to make court-ordered repairs, the City can make the repairs and bill the landlord.

-

Continuing to enforce Chow’s Renovictions By-Law

-

Passing a Maximum Temperature By-Law

-

Continuing to advocate for real rent control to the province
