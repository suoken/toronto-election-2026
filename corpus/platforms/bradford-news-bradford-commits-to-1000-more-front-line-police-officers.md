Title: BRADFORD COMMITS TO 1,000 MORE FRONT LINE POLICE OFFICERS
Published: 2026-07-23
Issue category: Crime

TORONTO, ON | Speaking outside Toronto Police Headquarters this morning, Brad Bradford announced that as Mayor he will hire 1,000 net new front line police officers over his first term.

"A city that is not safe is not free," said Bradford. "Toronto has grown by 21 per cent since 2010, with virtually the same number of officers on our streets. Think about what that math means for the 911 call that matters most in your life. I won't accept it, and the people of this city shouldn't have to."

The 1,000 new officers will be front line positions, on patrol, in neighbourhoods, and at subway stations. This exceeds the Toronto Police Service’s current multi-year hiring plan, which projects a net increase of just 250 net new officers deployed between the end of 2026 and 2029.

“The current plan is not catching up. It is falling behind more slowly,” said Bradford. “These will be officers on patrol, in your neighbourhood, at your subway station. Not administrators, and not another layer of management.”

Bradford’s community safety plan also includes visible policing at every subway station, targeted enforcement on repeat violent offenders, personally serving on the Police Services Board as Mayor, and continued advocacy to Queen’s Park and Ottawa for bail reform.

BACKGROUNDER

-
In 2010, Toronto had 5,556 officers and a ratio of 208 police officers per 100,000 residents. By 2018, that dropped to 4,705 officers and a ratio of 161 officers per 100,000 residents. At the end of 2026, Toronto is expected to reach 5,685 officers and a ratio of 171 to 100,000. (Source: 2025 TPS Budget Note)

-
Toronto’s ratio of 171 officers is below that of cities such as Edmonton (~194), Vancouver (~200), Montreal (~220), New York (~322), London UK (~370) and Sydney, Australia (~200).
