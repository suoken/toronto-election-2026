Title: BRADFORD CALLS FOR URGENT ACTION TO PROTECT TORONTO JOBS FROM NEW U.S. TARIFFS
Published: 2026-07-22
Issue category: Cost of Living

New 50 per cent tariffs threaten Toronto industries

TORONTO – July 22, 2026 – Mayoral candidate Brad Bradford supports urgent action by Ottawa, Queen's Park and City Hall to quickly protect Toronto workers and manufacturers from the latest round of U.S. tariffs.

On July 20, U.S. President Donald Trump announced additional 50 per cent tariffs on approximately $20 billion worth of Canadian goods, effective August 19. The tariffs will apply even to goods that meet CUSMA rules of origin. Targeted products include clothing, furniture, cement, dairy, wine and hockey equipment, putting Toronto manufacturing jobs and local suppliers directly at risk.

The damage has already started. Toronto apparel makers that design and manufacture their products here have paused shipments to American customers. Furniture manufacturers and building-material suppliers that serve customers on both sides of the border are now assessing their exposure.

"Donald Trump is picking another fight with Canada, and Toronto workers shouldn't be left to pay the bill," said Bradford. "The people who cut, sew, build and ship Canadian products need action from every level of government. Ottawa and Queen's Park must help businesses losing orders. City Hall must cut costs and delays and use its purchasing power to back Canadian-made goods."

City Hall's response so far dates to March 2025, when Council approved an economic action plan built for that year's 25 per cent tariffs. Its centrepiece for manufacturers was a six-month property-tax deferral that expired last November. The tariffs got worse. The help ran out.

Toronto doesn't set American trade policy. But City Hall does control the costs, delays and red tape manufacturers face at home, and right now those are pushing in the wrong direction. Cities across North America are competing hard for the employers Toronto risks losing. When a manufacturer weighs where to invest, expand or relocate, every month of permitting delay and every unnecessary cost counts against this city.

"The tariff war isn’t over. City Hall can’t go to sleep. We must continue to pursue bold action in the face of these unfair actions," said Bradford. "These tariffs are a test of how competitive Toronto really is. We can't control what Washington does, but we can control whether this is the best place in North America to make things. Every barrier we leave standing is a reason for the next company to build somewhere else."

Bradford is calling for:

-
Immediate federal and provincial assistance for Canadian businesses losing or at risk of losing American orders.

-
A City of Toronto tariff-response table with affected manufacturers, workers and industry representatives.

-
Faster municipal approvals for manufacturers expanding or modernizing Toronto facilities.

-
More City purchasing of Toronto- and Canadian-made products wherever trade rules permit.

-
Real property-tax relief, not another temporary deferral, for industrial properties and qualifying small businesses.

-
Faster removal of interprovincial trade and regulatory barriers.

-
Support for Toronto manufacturers seeking new customers across Canada and around the world.

At City Council, Bradford has introduced motions proposing 25 per cent property-tax relief for approximately 3,500 industrial businesses and 30,000 qualifying small businesses, leaving local employers with more money to invest and hire. Unlike the City's expired deferral, that money stays with businesses instead of coming due later. That relief matters even more now.

Bradford’s proposed tariff-response table would bring manufacturers and workers directly to City Hall to track lost orders and halted shipments, identify what the City can fix, and make the case for further help from Ottawa and Queen's Park.

"The United States is making it harder for Canadian companies to sell across the border. We shouldn't make it harder for them to sell across Canada," said Bradford. "A Toronto manufacturer that loses American sales should be able to find new customers in other provinces without a different set of rules at every border."

Companies that choose to make their products in Toronto should be able to keep making them here. Every level of government should be working to keep those companies, and the jobs they provide, in Canada.
