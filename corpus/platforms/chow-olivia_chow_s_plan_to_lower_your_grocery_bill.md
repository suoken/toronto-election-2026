Olivia Chow's Plan to Lower Your Grocery Bill

Chow will stop big grocers from using your data to charge you more, and help local grocers keep prices down

TORONTO— Ahead of Thanksgiving, Olivia Chow announced her plan to lower grocery bills by banning surveillance pricing, cutting taxes for local grocers and bringing more stores closer to home.

Joined by families at Fresh Picked, a local independent grocery store, Chow said Toronto should use the tools at its disposal to bring costs down for families and protect shoppers from unfair pricing.

“People are paying more and more at the grocery store. In every corner of the city, people are asking if the City can help. They want and deserve a mayor that stands up for them,” said Chow. “I don’t want parents to make impossible choices at checkout because they can’t afford it. We have to use every power at our disposal to keep grocery prices fair and encourage more independent grocery stores to open, so we can give people more affordable options. That’s how we lower your grocery bill.”

Chow’s plan to Lower Your Grocery Bill will:

-

Ban surveillance pricing on groceries. Chow will bring a by-law to the first Council meeting of the new term to prohibit grocers from using shoppers’ personal information to set individualized prices.

-

Cut taxes for independent and community grocery stores. Chow will work with the Province to establish a property tax discount for independent and community grocers, helping neighbourhood businesses compete with the big chains.

-

Help open more community grocery stores. Chow will help connect community grocers with vacant storefronts on Toronto’s main streets, through the City’s economic development department.

-

Fight laws that stop competition. Chow will work with the Province to end Restrictive Covenants that prevent competing grocers from opening in big chains’ plazas or former locations.

The plan builds on Chow’s work saving families on grocers by expanding the school food programs, and her commitment to deliver universal school lunches.

Chow said affordability is a clear choice in this election.

“Making life more affordable matters to me,” said Chow. “I will keep standing up for families and working to bring costs down. Let’s keep building a Toronto that is safe, caring and affordable.”
