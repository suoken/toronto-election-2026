Title: BRADFORD: END THE REGISTRATION SPRINT
Published: 2026-06-03
Issue category: Cost of Living

Plan would replace Toronto's pre-dawn dash for summer camp with a fair, modern, family-first system

TORONTO: Every spring, tens of thousands of Torontonians set an alarm for 6:30 a.m., log onto a highly sought-after registration portal, and press refresh. If they’re smart, they have a laptop, iPad and cell phone all going at the same time – tripling their chances. If it’s a two adult household, both are doing it at the same time on separate devices. Are they trying to secure a ticket to the world cup game or a Taylor Swift concert? No. They’re trying to crack the Toronto Recreation Summer Camp sign-up. The “Hunger Games” of City Hall registration.

Signing your children up for a City of Toronto-run summer camp has become an Olympic skill, with families sharing tricks on WhatsApp groups and over text messages. Within minutes, the most sought-after spots are always gone. Hours later, parents are still in hidden queues, watching the screen, hoping the session at their local community centre has not vanished.

It would be comical, if it didn’t eat up hours away from what matters most, and didn’t leave families high and dry come the summer months. This is the time tax. Hours stolen from families before they have even started their day. Time they will never get back, levied by a system the Mayor’s Office refuses to fix.

Today Mayoral candidate Brad Bradford announced a plan to end the Registration Sprint and replace it with a system built for the families it is supposed to serve.

“The City of Toronto has built a registration system that punishes the parents who need it most. A single mother working a morning shift cannot sit at a screen at 7 a.m. A new family in Scarborough does not know which catchment they are in, or that they had to pre-register. The Mayor has made her peace with families racing the clock at sunrise. I have not.”

-Brad Bradford

Fixing this isn’t like cracking the DaVinci code. There are jurisdictions that have figured it out: Washington, D.C. has a two-week registration window for summer camps in which every family has the same chance regardless of when they apply in that window. New York City uses the same model. Philadelphia rolled out a lottery at its highest-demand sites earlier this year. Bermuda announced its lottery in March. The technology exists. The model is proven. Toronto can do this.

Bradford's plan to end the Registration Sprint has three priorities.

1. A FAIR START. A multi-day registration window, followed by a transparent draw. No advantage to anyone with faster internet, an earlier alarm, or fewer obligations between 6:30 and 7:30 a.m. Sibling registrations can be linked, so brothers and sisters are placed together. Independent oversight of the draw, with results published.

2. LOCAL ACCESS. Expand staggered registration by district and program category, so the parent in Etobicoke is not bidding against the parent in East York for the same gym at the same minute. Grow the Early Local Registration Initiative to all 37 free community recreation centres, providing priority access for the families who live closest.

3. A SYSTEM THAT WORKS FOR FAMILIES. Reorder your wishlist after you have built it. See your real position on the waitlist. Prove your Toronto residency once and carry it forward. See your Welcome Policy subsidy at checkout, automatically applied. A staffed help line during peak windows.

Recreation registration is, at its core, a cost of living issue. When parents aren’t able to access city-run programming, the alternative is a private day camp at three or four times the price. Families pay the time tax in lost hours, and pay it again in dollars when the system fails to deliver.

“I am the father of two young girls. My wife and I know how broken the system is, first-hand. But this is not about my family. It is about every family in this city.

Other cities have already figured this out. Toronto can too. If I am fortunate enough to lead this city, you will have a mayor who fixes the small systems that make everyday life work. Because families are the bedrock of this city, and they deserve a city that is built for them.” -Brad Bradford
