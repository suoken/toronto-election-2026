Title: Bradford Pledges to Make Food More Affordable and Accessible for Toronto Families
Published: 2026-09-14
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

Toronto mayoral candidate says no child in this city should go to school hungry, and that City Hall must do more to ease the rising costs facing Toronto families

TORONTO — Mayoral candidate Brad Bradford today reaffirmed his commitment to tackling the rising cost of living in Toronto, pledging that as mayor he would protect the Student Nutrition Program and take on the restrictive covenants that create “food deserts” in Toronto neighbourhoods.

"It’s unacceptable that in a country as wealthy as Canada, kids are going to school on an empty stomach," said Bradford. "I support the city’s Student Nutrition Program as part of the federal government’s move to make the National School Food Program permanent. As mayor, I’ll make sure Toronto is a full partner in getting that support into our schools, not a bottleneck. A hungry child isn’t a school problem or a city problem. It’s everyone’s problem."

Bradford said that under his leadership, the City of Toronto would:

-
Work directly with the province and school boards to ensure Toronto schools are maximizing their share of federal school food funding and benefiting from the Student Nutrition Program;

-
Cut red tape that slows down getting food programs into schools and community hubs that need them most;

-
Partner with community agencies and local growers to increase economic and community development benefits from the program; and

-
Report publicly on the program’s rollout and impact in Toronto, so families can see the funding translating into food in the hands of our kids.

The rising cost of living has forced too many Toronto families into difficult choices just to make ends meet.

"Every level of government has a role to play in making life affordable in our city. But under Mayor Chow, costs have gone up and taxes have risen nearly 20% in 3 years," Bradford said. "Toronto is stuck paying more and getting less. There’s more the City can do to lower costs for families, and that starts with change at City Hall."

Bradford also announced a commitment to address restrictive covenants in the grocery sector: private legal clauses, often attached to a property’s title when a grocery chain sells or leases a site, that bar any future occupant from operating a grocery store or supermarket.

Research from the Competition Bureau and the Public Interest Advocacy Centre has identified dozens of these covenants across Canadian cities, including in Ontario, creating "food deserts" where neighbourhoods lose walkable access to affordable groceries simply because a competitor is legally barred from moving in.

"This isn’t about zoning or red tape. It’s a private contract sitting quietly on a piece of land, keeping a grocery store out and prices high for the people who live nearby," said Bradford. "If we’re serious about making food more affordable and accessible in this city, we have to deal with the barriers nobody’s talking about, not just the obvious ones."

Bradford said that, as mayor, he would:

-
Formally press the province to adopt Manitoba-style legislation voiding anti-competitive restrictive covenants and exclusivity clauses in the grocery sector, working in coalition with other Ontario municipalities facing food deserts;

-
Ensure the City never attaches restrictions limiting grocery use on any City-owned land it sells or leases for commercial development; and

-
Use the City's economic development and planning tools to actively court independent and discount grocers into covenant-free sites in underserved neighbourhoods.
