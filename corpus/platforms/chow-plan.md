Protecting Services

Olivia Chow will protect the services people rely on.

Bradford's agenda of cuts will takes us backwards to the days of closing libraries, raising transit fares and slashing city services.

This election is a choice.

Olivia knows how hard people are working to build a life here. She believes the City should work for you. Better transit, lowering your costs, improving safety and building more homes you can afford.

In these times, your mayor should protect the services we need most. Let's move forward, together.

10 rides, then free: Lowering TTC costs

Olivia will make your commute more affordable: After 10 paid TTC rides, the rest of the week is free. She will also keep TTC fares frozen for the fourth year straight.

Previous administrations raised fares and cut service. As mayor, Olivia did the opposite. She froze fares for three years, while investing in more service and more maintenance. Her plan will make the TTC more affordable and reliable.

Read more about Olivia's plan to lower TTC costs

Lowering grocery bills

People are paying too much at the grocery store. They need a mayor that will stand up for them.

Olivia will use every power at her disposal to make grocery prices fairer and encourage more independent grocery stores to open.

Olivia's plan to Lower Your Grocery Bill:

-

Ban "surveillance pricing" on groceries, to stop big chains from using your personal data to set higher prices

-

Cut taxes for independent and community grocery stores, helping neighbourhood shops compete with big chains

-

Help open more community grocery stores by connecting grocers with vacant storefronts on main streets

-

Fight laws that stop competition and favour big chains

- Feeding more kids in schools, saving families on their grocery bill each week

Read more about how Olivia will Lower Your Grocery Bill

Keeping neighbourhoods safe

Olivia has a holistic view of community safety, investing in both emergency services and community services to support youth.

As mayor, she’s hired more front-line emergency service workers: police, firefighters, paramedics and mental health crisis workers. 911 call wait times are down 90%.

Olivia will invest more in youth violence prevention programs that work, providing mentorship, job opportunities and recreation. She’s committed to making communities stronger and supporting young people, helping them down the right path.

Read more about Olivia's plan to keep neighbourhoods safe

Creating more jobs

Olivia Chow will create more jobs in Toronto by attracting private investment, and building more housing and transit.

In her time as mayor, Olivia has brought in more investment. Recently, Hitachi invested $30 million to create 1,200 jobs in Scarborough, and Toyota is expanding their headquarters for another 1,000 jobs. Sanofi, Unilever and other big companies have invested here, showing confidence in our city despite these uncertain times.

Chow's plan for more jobs:

-

Create 15,000 New Youth Jobs through the Toronto Youth Employment Strategy, in partnership with the United Way. This partnership has already created 2,600 jobs in just a few months.

-

Create more local and youth jobs for Scarborough: By using Community Benefits Agreements, guaranteeing that 20% of construction jobs on the Scarborough East LRT project go to local residents, with a focus on youth - and a target 15% of apprentices on the project for local youth aged 18 to 35. This is double the local hiring commitments made on the Finch West and Eglinton Crosstown LRTs.

-

Create 37,000 new construction jobs by building another 10,000 rental homes through her Rental Housing Supply Program. Her commitment builds on housing programs already delivering more than 15,000 homes under construction now. See here.

- Continuing working with the Mayor’s Economic Action Team, a group of business and labour leaders that help navigate the trade war. Her plan has already secured $220 million in private investment to expand manufacturing in Toronto, supporting 1,200 jobs.

Olivia Chow will continue working to attract more private investment in Toronto.

Less traffic and congestion

There’s nothing more frustrating than being stuck in traffic, whether you’re in a car or on transit. That’s time you’ll never get back.

That’s why Olivia has a plan to get Toronto moving with practical changes to our roads and by investing in transit.

Getting our roads moving:

-

Better technology with smart AI traffic lights and intelligent intersections that use real-time data to manage the flow of cars and buses.

-

Better coordination for construction, road closures and major events, and implementing the traffic learnings from the FIFA World Cup.

-

Upgrading the City’s Traffic Command Centre, building a new command centre for real-time traffic operations across the city.

-

Hiring traffic agents to direct traffic and keep intersections clear

Getting transit moving:

-

Investing in more service: before Olivia, transit service was cut and fares went up. She did the opposite and increased service while keeping fares low. She will keep increasing service.

-

Building the Scarborough East LRT to help Scarborough transit riders move faster.

-

Transit signal priority now saves Line 5 and 6 riders 10-20 minutes per day, she will expand it to Spadina, King and Queens Quay.

-

Make transit more affordable: 10 rides and it’s free, each week. See here.

As mayor, Olivia has made practical changes to reduce congestion. She sped up Gardiner Expressway construction so it re-opened 18 months ahead of schedule. She built the Scarborough Busway to get buses out of mixed traffic and saving riders an hour per week. And she’s created incentives for construction companies to get out of our roads faster.

There’s more work to do. Olivia Chow has a plan to get Toronto moving.

Better transit for Scarborough

Olivia stands up for Scarborough. She'll deliver better transit for Scarborough TTC riders:

-

Lower transit costs: 10 trips then the TTC is free, each week. Lowering the cost of transit can save Scarborough TTC riders hundreds of dollars a year while reducing congestion.

-

Building the Scarborough East LRT: Chow will get shovels in the ground next term. She has already saved nearly $400 million for a downpayment, and she has expedited design and engineering work. Chow has made it the City’s top transit priority.

-

Investing in more frequent bus service for Scarborough. Throughout her time as mayor, Chow has invested in increasing TTC service levels in Scarborough and she will continue. She will never cut transit.

-

Keeping her promise to build the busway: Olivia stood up for Scarborough after the SRT derailed and built the Busway. It’s open now and saves 100,000 Scarborough transit riders an hour per week. The temporary red bus lanes on Kennedy and Midland will now be removed.

-

New subway cars on the Scarborough Subway: Chow is buying new state-of-the-art subway trains for Line 2, which will serve the new Scarborough Subway when it opens, giving riders a reliable ride.

Addressing homelessness and drug use

No one should be living on the street, or using drugs on transit or parks. Our public spaces belong to everyone, and everyone deserves safe shelter and a chance to get well.

Olivia Chow has a plan to bring people off the streets:

-

Building more supportive housing: 600 more beds with drug treatment and mental health services

-

Building more homeless shelters: 6 more shelters are under construction right now, with 1,600 more beds in the pipeline.

-

Working with residents, businesses, BIAs and community groups to enhance response in hot-spot areas of public drug use

-

Continue reducing encampments, which are already down 70% across the city

-

Work with the Provincial government, which is responsible for health care, on expanding mental health and addiction services in Toronto.

We also need to prevent homelessness in the first place. Last year, 4,000 people were prevented from becoming homeless because Olivia expanded City programs that help prevent evictions.

Olivia will do more to bring people off the street and address the addictions crisis.

Cracking down on bad landlords

Olivia will establish a Bad Landlords Task Force to go after the city’s worst landlords and fix apartments so tenants can live in dignity.

- More enforcement officers and lawyers to inspect units and take landlords to court.

- For landlords that refuse to repair their buildings, the City will go in to make repairs and bill the landlord.

Olivia stands up for renters. She passed a Renovictions By-law to stop illegal evictions, doubled funding to programs that prevent evictions, and doubled the number of RentSafe Officers inspecting buildings.

Read more about Olivia's plan to crack down on bad landlords

Building more homes

Housing is the biggest cost most people face each month. For decades, the City walked away from building housing. Olivia Chow is reversing that generational mistake.

Olivia will build more homes that people can afford.

Right now, 15,000 city-supported units are under construction. She will get shovels in the ground on 10,000 more next year. Her plan will build 65,000 affordable and rent-controlled homes.

That's how we bring the price of rent down and stop the skyrocketing cost of housing in our city.

Read more about Olivia Chow's plan to build more homes.

Moratorium on data centres

Data Centres have a big impact on our resources, drive up energy bills and disrupt neighbourhoods.

Olivia Chow will stop them. We need to move forward with affordability and the wellbeing of Torontonians at the centre of our decision making.

Her opponent this election wants more data centres in Toronto. He said so.

Feeding kids in school

Olivia will provide lunch in schools to give parents grocery relief. Families could save up to $1,800 per child every year.

As mayor, Olivia expanded the school food program to every school, providing at least a mid-morning meal to 330,000 kids. Her plan is to expand the program to provide lunch in every school to help lower your costs.

Olivia will lower your grocery bills and give kids the best start.

A more senior-friendly city

Olivia will make it easier for seniors to age at home and stay social.

Her plan includes more support at home, more mobile clinics and more access to recreation.

- Expanding Toronto’s free homemaking services helps with cleaning, laundry, meal prep, and household basics.

- Expanding mobile dental and primary care clinics in neighbourhoods

- Expanding recreation opportunities for seniors

Physical and social wellbeing so you can stay in your home longer.

More family fun

Olivia will put children’s programming in every library, every weekend, and expand the number of free activities in the Museum and Attraction Pass.

An average day out with your family can cost hundreds of dollars, Olivia will make it easier to take advantage of what our city has to offer, for free.

With more library programs and more Museum and Attractions Passes, Olivia will provide more places to take the kids.

Local democracy matters: No strong mayor powers

Olivia will never override City Council by using strong mayor powers. She believes local democracy and local voices matter.

Other candidates have said they would use strong mayor powers to override communities and City Council. That's wrong.

As Mayor, Olivia is committed to listening to Torontonians. Olivia has been steadfast in her commitment to not use strong mayor powers, and that will not change.
