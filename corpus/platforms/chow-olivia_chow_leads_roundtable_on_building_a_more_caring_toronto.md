Olivia Chow Leads Roundtable on Building a More Caring Toronto

Prominent Toronto women highlight what’s at stake in this election

TORONTO — Olivia Chow joined prominent women leaders from across Toronto in "Women Talking – Building a More Caring Toronto," in a roundtable discussion led by Supriya Dwivedi, on the future of Canada's largest city. See the video here.

The event brought together influential figures including former Premier Kathleen Wynne, former Mayor Barbara Hall, actor Jean Yoon and business leader Cheesan Chew to highlight the choice facing voters in Toronto’s mayoral election.

"This election is a choice between moving forward together to make our city more caring, or going backwards with cuts to the services families depend on," said Chow. "I am proud to have the support of some of Toronto’s strongest leaders as we build a city that supports women of all ages and their families.”

The roundtable highlighted key priorities for the city, including: protecting public services like public libraries and our water supply; investing in local neighbourhoods; and defending democratic values by championing inclusive leadership.

Participants in the roundtable included prominent women who endorse Olivia Chow for mayor:

-

Kathleen Wynne — 25th Premier of Ontario: First female Premier of Ontario and former MPP for Don Valley West, recognized for investments in public transit, education, social policy, and LGBTQ+ advocacy.

-

Barbara Hall — 61st Mayor of Toronto: Former Toronto Mayor and Chief Commissioner of the Ontario Human Rights Commission, dedicated to human rights, social equity, and urban renewal.

-

Jean Yoon — Actor & Playwright: Award-winning Canadian actor and cultural advocate, best known for her role as "Umma" in Kim's Convenience.

-

Cheesan Chew — Entrepreneur & business leader: Over two decades building businesses and driving innovation in Toronto. Past president of the Canadian Club.

-

Supriya Dwivedi — Host: Respected political commentator and host known for analysis on urban affairs and public policy.
