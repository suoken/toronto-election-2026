Title: BRADFORD WILL GET SCARBOROUGH MOVING BY BUILDING REAL RAPID TRANSIT AND REMOVING RED BUS LANES: "SCARBOROUGH WAS PROMISED AN LRT. IT GOT RED PAINT."
Published: 2026-10-07
Issue category: Toronto Mayoral Campaign 2026

TORONTO, ON | Standing on Kingston Road in Scarborough this morning, Brad Bradford committed to removing the red bus lanes on Kingston Road, Morningside Avenue, Kennedy Road and Midland Road, and returning 12 kilometres of Scarborough road to everyone who uses it.

The red lanes were never meant to be the plan. They were painted in the fall of 2020 as a stand-in for the Eglinton East LRT. The City's own report said so: the corridor was chosen because consultation for the LRT was already done, and a bus lane "would help build ridership for a future LRT alignment." The lanes were the bridge. The LRT was supposed to follow.

6 years later, there is no LRT. There is no provincial funding. There is no federal funding. There is no construction date. In April, the province said the line isn't on the province's priority list. NDP Mayor Chow's answer was a new name, a $10 million design budget and a working group. In the same stretch of months, the waterfront's LRT received $3 billion in committed funding. Scarborough received a rebrand, and the red paint stayed.

"Scarborough was promised an LRT. It got red paint," said Bradford. "The lanes were supposed to be temporary. They were supposed to build ridership for a train that was coming. The train never came, because this Mayor never fought for it. So the temporary fix became permanent, and the people stuck in traffic beside an empty red lane are paying for a promise City Hall broke."

Toronto drivers lost 100 hours to congestion last year, more than 4 full days, and it got worse than the year before. In Scarborough, where distances are long and transit options are thin, the time tax hits hardest. It's the parent who misses bedtime because of traffic on Morningside. It's the shift worker on Kingston Road who leaves an hour early and still clocks in late. It's the UTSC student who spends more time getting to campus than in class. Nobody voted for that. Nobody at City Hall can tell them where the time went.

As mayor, Bradford will direct City staff and the TTC to immediately remove the red bus lanes on Kennedy Road and Midland Road, which are now redundant. He will also remove the red bus lanes on the Kingston Road and Morningside Avenue, changing those lanes to High-Occupancy Vehicle (HOV) lanes. Brad’s plan will maintain the red lanes on Eglinton Avenue East because it has more buses than any other road in Scarborough.

He will keep this plan in place until the Eglinton East LRT is funded and under construction, and he has already committed to making Scarborough’s LRT Toronto's top transit priority. As mayor, Bradford will make a personal funding push to Queen's Park and Ottawa in his first 100 days with a commitment to get shovels in the ground by 2030. Scarborough gets its road back now and its train for real, instead of paint forever and a train never.

Scarborough residents pay more and get less. NDP Mayor Chow raised property taxes nearly 20 per cent, the largest in the city's history, and if she's re-elected she'll raise them another 20 per cent at least. For that, Scarborough got red paint on the road and a rename on a map.

"Olivia Chow stood on the debate stage and declared that Toronto has reached its full potential," said Bradford. "Tell that to someone sitting in traffic on Kingston Road at 5:30 on a Tuesday, looking at an empty red lane that was supposed to be a train by now. Toronto isn't broken. It's stuck. And nowhere is it more stuck than in Scarborough."

"We're on the cusp of change. Change is within our grasp," said Bradford. "To change the city, we need to change the mayor. On October 26th, Scarborough can choose a mayor who gets it moving. Not later. Right now."
