# Toronto 2026 Municipal Election — Voter Guide: Wards 14–25

Election day: **Monday, October 26, 2026**, 10 a.m.–8 p.m. Toronto's election is non-partisan; the candidate with the most votes in each ward wins (first-past-the-post). Nominations closed August 21, 2026.

**How this section was built:** Ward-by-ward candidate lists are taken from the compilation on the Wikipedia page "2026 Toronto municipal election" (last reviewed Oct 2026), which cites the City of Toronto's certified candidate list as its source for every name marked [11]. The City's own elections candidate directory at toronto.ca/elections could not be read directly via text fetch (the guessed URL returned a 404), so readers should confirm final certification at the City Clerk's official list. Voting records are drawn from City of Toronto published council decisions/minutes (secure.toronto.ca) with recorded votes, and from reputable news outlets. All claims are attributed; gaps are flagged, not filled.

## Key motions referenced — quick guide

All vote counts below are the City Clerk's recorded votes from secure.toronto.ca unless noted.

1. **Bike lanes — Bill 212 response (2024.CC23.1, Nov 13–14, 2024).** Council voted 21–4 to oppose the province's Bill 212 amendments stripping municipal control of bike lanes and to direct the City Solicitor to explore litigation (Chow motion, parts 1–5). A separate motion by Brad Bradford to support restoring two vehicle lanes on Bloor St W (Runnymede–Resurrection) even if it required removing bike lanes lost 6–18.
2. **Property tax — 2025 budget levy (2025.CC27.1, Feb 11, 2025).** Council adopted the tax rates funding Mayor Chow's 2025 budget 19–5 (No: Bradford, Burnside, Holyday, Pasternak, Perruzza; Saxe and Crisanti absent). Residential increase: 5.4% + 1.5% City Building Fund levy = 6.9% total (Storeys; City decision text).
3. **TTC budget — 2025 budget, TTC portion (2025.CC27.1, Feb 11, 2025).** Adopted 22–1 (only Holyday opposed; Saxe absent, Colle declared an interest, Crisanti absent).
4. **TTC service — RapidTO transit priority lanes, Dufferin & Bathurst (2025.EX25.4, Jul 23–24, 2025).** Dedicated bus/streetcar lanes adopted 20–3 (No: Bradford, Holyday, Pasternak; Cheng and Colle declared interests and did not vote). On the Bathurst-specific recommendations (3–4), the vote was 18–5, with Burnside joining the No votes.
5. **Housing — Rental Renovation Licence (renoviction) by-law (2024.PH16.4, Nov 13–14, 2024).** Adopted 25–1 (only Holyday opposed); all ward-14–25 incumbents in office at the time voted Yes. Effective July 31, 2025.
6. **Housing — as-of-right sixplex pilot (2025.PH22.4, Jun 25–26, 2025).** Council adopted zoning to allow low-rise sixplexes as-of-right on a pilot basis (Toronto & East York District plus specified Scarborough lands) 18–6. No votes: Crisanti, Holyday, **Kandavel**, Pasternak, Perruzza, **Thompson**. A Holyday deferral motion lost 17–7 (Burnside voted Yes to defer).
7. **Shelter — six new shelter sites (2025.PH23.3, Jul 23–24, 2025).** Council approved zoning amendments for six new municipal shelter sites (68 Sheppard Ave W, 1615 Dufferin St, 2204–2212 Eglinton Ave W / 601 Caledonia Rd, 2535 Gerrard St E, 66–66Y Third St, 1220–1222 Wilson Ave) 22–3 (No: Bradford, Holyday, Pasternak). All ward-14–25 incumbents in office voted Yes; the Ward 25 seat was vacant.
8. **2026 budget (Feb 10, 2026).** Council passed Mayor Chow's $18.9B operating budget with a 2.2% combined residential property-tax increase (0.7% operating + 1.5% City Building Fund) and a third consecutive TTC fare freeze. Reported as unanimous (Toronto Sentinel; Downsview Advocate); City of Toronto news release confirms adoption.
