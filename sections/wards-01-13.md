# Toronto 2026 Municipal Election — Voter Guide: Wards 1–13 (City Councillor)

**Election day: Monday, October 26, 2026 (10 a.m.–8 p.m.).** Nominations closed August 21, 2026; the City Clerk certified 361 candidates (53 mayoral, 190 councillor, 118 trustee) on August 24, 2026.

**How to read this section.** Each ward lists its certified councillor candidates. Candidates are listed alphabetically by surname; incumbents seeking re-election are marked **(incumbent)**. For incumbents (and any candidate with council voting history), a compact table records their votes on key headline motions from Oct 2024–Oct 2026, taken from the City Clerk's published recorded votes (secure.toronto.ca council agenda items). First-time candidates are marked "no council voting record — first-time candidate." Stated positions are included **only** when verified from a candidate's campaign materials or a documented candidate-comparison source; otherwise marked "no public position found." Nothing here is invented. This guide compares — it does not recommend or endorse any candidate.

**Key motions used in the voting tables** (all from City of Toronto published council decisions with recorded votes):

| # | Motion | Date | Item |
|---|--------|------|------|
| A | Oppose Ontario Bill 212 (bike-lane removal) amendments — carried 21–4 | Nov 13–14, 2024 | 2024.CC23.1 motion 1 |
| B | Support restoring 2 car lanes each way on Bloor St W (Runnymede–Resurrection) even if bike lanes removed — **lost** 6–18 | Nov 13–14, 2024 | 2024.CC23.1 motion 3 |
| C | 2025 budget: adopt property tax rates (5.4% residential + 1.5% City Building Fund = **6.9%**) — carried 19–5 | Feb 11, 2025 | 2025.CC27.1 |
| D | 2026 budget: adopt property tax rates (0.7% residential + 1.5% City Building Fund = **2.2%**) — carried 19–5 (revote) | Feb 10, 2026 | 2026.CC38.1 |

| I | RapidTO transit-priority lanes (Dufferin & Bathurst) — carried 20–3 | Jul 23–24, 2025 | 2025.EX25.4 |

**A note on the 2026 budget:** some media reported the 2026 budget as "unanimous," but the City Clerk's recorded vote shows the property-tax-rate portion (item D above) passed 19–5, with Crisanti, Holyday, Pasternak and Perruzza voting no (plus Brad Bradford, Ward 19, who was still on council). The TTC portion (K) did pass 23–0 with no opposition.

## Notes, gaps, and caveats

- **Candidate lists:** verified against the City Clerk's certified list as republished by Wikipedia (citing the official list; page crawled Oct 19, 2026) and spot-checked against votetoronto.com and onac.ca ward pages. The City's own candidate-list page (toronto.ca) is JavaScript-driven and could not be text-extracted by the available tools. Confirm the final ballot at toronto.ca/elections or via MyVote.
- **Voting records:** all votes above are from the City Clerk's published recorded votes on secure.toronto.ca (council agenda item histories). "Yes"/"No" reflects the councillor's vote on the motion as adopted or defeated; "Absent" means not recorded as voting (may include late arrivals/departures).
- **Ward 4 and Ward 11** have no incumbent running; all candidates are marked as first-time council candidates (Mike Layton previously served 2010–2022 but has no votes in the Oct 2024–Oct 2026 window).
- **Challenger positions:** verified only where stated above with a named source (campaign sites or documented candidate comparisons). All other challengers are marked "no public position found" — campaign materials were not systematically reviewed for every candidate. Endorsements listed are documented public endorsements (Labour Council, Progress Toronto, ABC Toronto, CUPE Local 79, ATU Local 113, No Demovictions) and are not positions.
- **Scope limits:** tables cover key headline motions only, not exhaustive roll-calls. Notable motions outside the required categories (e.g., the March 2025 councillor pay raise, the June 2025 protest "bubble zone" bylaw, the Nov 2024 shelter-infrastructure plan) were not included. Per-councillor votes on individual 2026-budget amendment motions beyond those shown are available in the agenda item histories linked below.
- **Context:** Brad Bradford (Ward 19) is running for mayor and vacated his council seat; Olivia Chow is the incumbent mayor seeking re-election.
